X-gate worked table for IBKRCommission (D4, D65) — Step 5 of VERIFICATION_SCHEME.md.
Companion to test_ibkr_commission.py; every row below is asserted there.

Schedule modeled: IBKR Fixed pricing, US stocks/ETFs.
  per_share             = USD 0.005
  min_per_order         = USD 1.00
  max_pct_of_trade_value = 1% (the cap OVERRIDES the minimum)

Formula: commission = min( max(0.005 × |shares|, 1.00), 0.01 × |shares| × price )

ANCHORING CAVEAT (honesty over comfort): the X-test is supposed to check against a
reference we didn't write. IBKR's pricing pages 403-block automated fetches (verified
during planning — both interactivebrokers.com URL forms refused). The constants above
are IBKR's long-published, widely-documented Fixed schedule, but they have NOT been
re-verified against the live page by this project. One-time manual check against
https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-stocks.php is still owed —
tracked in AITODO.md. Regulatory pass-throughs (SEC/FINRA fees on sells) are NOT
modeled; deferred per D65.

Spot-checks of the interesting rows:
  Row 6:  max(0.005×10, 1.00) = max(0.05, 1.00) = 1.00; cap = 0.01×10×0.50 = 0.05;
          min(1.00, 0.05) = 0.05. The published "maximum 1% of trade value" applies
          even below the $1 minimum — this is the row that distinguishes the correct
          min/cap ordering from the tempting-but-wrong max(min(...), 1.00) form.
  Row 7:  per-share pricing on a $200,000 notional order costs $1.00 — a flat-5bps
          model would charge $100. This is D4's "~70× divergence" rationale made
          concrete.
  Row 11: 0.005×200 = 1.00 = min = 0.01×200×0.50 — all three regimes coincide; any
          off-by-one in the formula's comparisons shows up here.

Also asserted: zero shares → zero commission (no minimum charged on a non-trade).
