Golden-master hand ledger for the futures cost bricks and the cost table (D591).
Companion to test_futures_costs_ledger.py — every number below has a matching assertion there.

Per CONTRIBUTING.md the arithmetic here was worked BEFORE the assertions were written, by a
calculator that never imports `backtest_framework`. The inputs are two kinds of thing and the
distinction is the whole point of the table this file gates:

  (a) MEASURED numbers, copied out of a committed JSON artefact by key path. They are quoted
      here to their full repr, never retyped to fewer digits, because R16 asks for a published
      number to reproduce EXACTLY on the build that produced it.
  (b) DECLARED numbers — commission — which nothing here measured. They are brokerage
      assumptions carried in a runner's source literal, and they are labelled as such.

Reading a committed artefact is not importing the codebase: the artefact is the published
number. What this file never does is take a figure from the module under test.


0. THE TWO BRICK RULES
================================================================================
Both charge PER FILL, and a round trip is two fills of one contract.

    FuturesCommission(per_round_trip_usd = C)
        cost(instrument, quantity, price) = 0.5 * C * |quantity|          [price ignored]

    TickCrossing(ticks_per_round_trip = T)
        cost(instrument, quantity, price) = 0.5 * T * tick_usd * |quantity|   [price ignored]

    round trip, one contract  =  cost(+1) + cost(-1)  =  C  +  T * tick_usd

0.5 is a power of two, so halving and re-doubling is exact in binary: the round trip is
`C + T*tick_usd` to the bit, not to a tolerance. That is asserted, not assumed.

Commission is charged PER CONTRACT (that is why it dominates at micro size — D469: $3 on a
$1.25 tick is 2.4 ticks, on a $12.50 tick it is 0.32). Crossing is charged per contract too,
because tick_usd is a one-contract quantity.

3. THE D556 RULE — cost per side = comm/2 + 0.5 tick, so a round trip is comm + one tick
================================================================================
The rule is `dollar_book` in run_d555_tsmom_replication.py:395-405, which D556 reuses:

    cost = sides * (comm_rt/2 + 0.5 * tick_usd)

so one round trip (two sides) is exactly `comm_rt + tick_usd`. In the vocabulary of §0 that is
TickCrossing(1.0): one full tick a round trip, half a tick a side. Commission is
COMMISSION_RT = {"micro": 3.0, "full": 6.0} (run_d555:56, "D468's convention"), and the size
charged is `min_size` from data/d556_carry_timing.json::dollar_book.min_size.

    root  min_size  comm    tick_usd   ROUND TRIP = comm + tick_usd     as the record prints it
    NQ      MNQ     3.00      0.50      3.50                            3.50   exact
    ES      MES     3.00      1.25      4.25                            4.25   exact
    CL      MCL     3.00      1.00      4.00                            4.00   exact
    SI      SIL     3.00      5.00      8.00                            8.00   exact
    ZN      ZN      6.00     15.625    21.625                           21.63  ROUNDED
    ZB      ZB      6.00     31.25     37.25                            37.25  exact
    ZF      ZF      6.00      7.8125   13.8125                          13.81  ROUNDED

  EXACT vs ROUNDED, and ZN is a genuine tie. 21.625 is exactly representable (173/8), so
  rounding it to two decimals is a tie at the third digit:
      half-up   -> 21.63     (the form the ledger and the brief print)
      half-even -> 21.62     (what Python's round(21.625, 2) returns)
  The test asserts the EXACT 21.625 and records both roundings. A record that quotes 21.63 is
  not reproducible by `round()` and that is worth knowing before someone "fixes" it.
      13.8125 -> 13.81 under both rules (the third digit is 2, no tie).

  MNQ TWO WAYS, and they disagree by design, not by error:
      D527 line   3.00 + 2.4110232735988832 * 0.50 = 4.205511636799441   (measured crossing)
      D556 rule   3.00 + 1.0                * 0.50 = 3.50                (one tick a round trip)
  The D556 rule is 0.7055116367994 cheaper. It is the seen ledger's convention, not a
  measurement, and the difference IS the measurement D527 made on that arm's own fills.

6. THE DISAGREEMENTS THE TABLE CARRIES, BECAUSE TWO LITERALS DIFFER
================================================================================
  (i) FULL-SIZE COMMISSION HAS TWO DECLARED VALUES.
        $4.00 / RT on ES   — scripts/d469_scalping_feasibility.py:83, from D258's "~$4-5"
        $6.00 / RT full    — scripts/run_d555_tsmom_replication.py:56, "D468's convention"
      The table's `full` entries carry 6.00 (the later, broader convention, and the one the
      seen ledger charges) and D469's 4.00 survives verbatim in ES's runner_lines.d469, which
      is what §4 reproduces against. Neither is measured. Picking one silently would make one
      of the two published bars un-reproducible.

  (ii) MICRO COMMISSION IS 3.00 EVERYWHERE — d469's MES, run_d555's "micro", D466's declared
       index-micro line. No disagreement to record; stated so that the agreement is on record.

  (iii) THE DEFINITION FILE'S tick_usd IS NOT IN DOLLARS FOR EVERY ROOT.
       data/fut_specs_from_definition.json computes tick_usd = tick_price_units *
       unit_of_measure_qty, which is dollars only where the price is quoted in dollars per
       unit. For the grains and livestock it is CENTS:
           ZC / ZS / ZW   1250.0   =  1250 cents  =  $12.50
           HE / LE        1000.0   =  1000 cents  =  $10.00
           ZL              600.0   =   600 cents  =   $6.00
       so those roots' D556 round-trip line reads 1256.0 / 1006.0 / 606.0 rather than $18.50 /
       $16.00 / $12.00. Within a root it is self-consistent — run_d555's gross uses the same
       price units — so it is a unit, not an error, and the table writes the artefact's number
       and FLAGS it rather than inventing a corrected one.
       SR3 is the opposite sign: 0.0625 where CME's tick is $6.25, a factor of 100 small; the
       percent-of-par divisor is applied to a uom_qty that is already dollars per index point.
       D555/D556 exclude SR3 from the dollar book (DOLLAR_EXCLUDED), so nothing published
       rests on it. Flagged, not corrected.

  (iii-a) SUPERSEDED 2026-09-22 BY D609 — CORRECTED, NOT FLAGGED. Amended in writing rather
       than edited, because the paragraph above is what D591 committed and what D604's record
       quotes.
       The reasoning above is right about the arithmetic and wrong about one sentence: "the
       table writes the artefact's number and FLAGS it rather than inventing a corrected one."
       A corrected number would not have been invented. data/fixtures/fut_breadth_hourly.meta.json
       has carried `tick_usd_full_contract` per root since D519, decided by a NOTIONAL test on
       the observed price, and it agrees with CME on all 17 roots where CME's own value is on
       file. The builder was reading the wrong field of the wrong file.
       D609 adds `tick_usd_full_contract`, `scaling_divisor`, `scaling_reason` and
       `tick_usd_raw_formula` to data/fut_specs_from_definition.json (the original `tick_usd`
       field is UNCHANGED, because this paragraph quotes it), and futures_cost_table.py now
       reads the corrected field. The table therefore carries:
           ZC / ZS / ZW   tick_usd  12.5    raw 1250.0    divisor 100
           HE / LE        tick_usd  10.0    raw 1000.0    divisor 100
           ZL             tick_usd   6.0    raw  600.0    divisor 100
           SR3            tick_usd   6.25   raw    6.25   divisor   1
       and the D556 round-trip line for those roots reads $18.50 / $16.00 / $12.00 as the
       paragraph above says it should.
       The claim that nothing PUBLISHED moved still holds, and it is checkable:
       data/d555_tsmom_replication.json#dollar_book.usd_per_point already reads ZC 50, ZS 50,
       ZW 50, ZL 600.0000000000001, LE 400, HE 400, SR3 2500 — the corrected values — because
       D555 read the breadth meta and not the definition file. Its bytes are unchanged by D609
       and a test asserts that.
       HG is why the divisor cannot come from `unit_of_measure`: HG and ZL are both "LBS" and
       differ by exactly 100, HG quoted in dollars per pound and ZL in cents.

8. WHAT THIS FILE DOES NOT ESTABLISH
================================================================================
- No strategy return, and nothing here reads a bar. Every input is a committed JSON artefact
  or a source literal.
- Commission is DECLARED. Nothing in this repository has measured a fill's commission, and
  the table says `"measured": false` on every commission row.
- The crossing censuses are all measured on 2025-09 .. 2026-09 and are applied by runners to
  2016-2023. D507 and D508 both say so in their own `limitations`: a tick is fixed in price
  terms, so a recent spread on an older, cheaper window is OPTIMISTIC. The table carries each
  line's window for exactly this reason and the default line is not a claim about 2016.
- The quoted spread (D507, D510) and the effective crossing (D508, D465) are DIFFERENT
  STATISTICS — a floor versus what an aggressor paid — and the table never averages them.
