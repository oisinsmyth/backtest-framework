Golden-master hand ledger for the futures cost bricks and the cost table.
Companion to test_futures_costs_ledger.py: every number below has a matching assertion there.

The arithmetic here was worked before the assertions were written, with a calculator that
never imports `backtest_framework`. Inputs are of two kinds:

  (a) MEASURED numbers: crossing costs in ticks, copied from data/futures_costs.json to their
      full repr, never retyped to fewer digits.
  (b) DECLARED numbers: commissions. Nothing here measured a commission; they are brokerage
      assumptions and the table labels them `"measured": false`.

Cost lines are named by what they measure ("one_tick", "effective_es_bp",
"effective_exec_hours", ...); data/futures_costs.json describes each one under `lines`.


0. THE TWO BRICK RULES
================================================================================
Both charge PER FILL, and a round trip is two fills of one contract.

    FuturesCommission(per_round_trip_usd = C)
        cost(instrument, quantity, price) = 0.5 * C * |quantity|          [price ignored]

    TickCrossing(ticks_per_round_trip = T)
        cost(instrument, quantity, price) = 0.5 * T * tick_usd * |quantity|   [price ignored]

    round trip, one contract  =  cost(+1) + cost(-1)  =  C  +  T * tick_usd

0.5 is a power of two, so halving and re-doubling is exact in binary: the round trip is
`C + T*tick_usd` to the bit, not to a tolerance.

Commission is charged per contract, which is why it dominates at micro size: $3 on a $1.25
tick is 2.4 ticks; on a $12.50 tick it is 0.32.


1. INSTRUMENT CONSTANTS
================================================================================
From data/futures_contract_specs.json (CME contract specifications). tick_usd =
usd_per_point * tick_points in every row, which `Future.__post_init__` re-checks.

    symbol   tick_points   usd_per_point   tick_usd
    ES         0.25            50.0          12.50
    MES        0.25             5.0           1.25
    NQ         0.25            20.0           5.00
    MNQ        0.25             2.0           0.50
    MCL        0.01           100.0           1.00
    MGC        0.1             10.0           1.00
    MNG        0.001         1000.0           1.00
    SIL        0.005         1000.0           5.00
    ZN         0.015625      1000.0          15.625
    ZB         0.03125       1000.0          31.25
    ZF         0.0078125     1000.0           7.8125

Every tick_usd here is a dyadic rational (1.25 = 5/4, 15.625 = 125/8, 7.8125 = 125/16), so
every product below is exact in binary. The two places that are about rounding say so.


2. MNQ WITH A MEASURED CROSSING
================================================================================
MEASURED crossing: 2.4110232735988832 ticks a round trip.
DECLARED commission: $3.00 a round trip.

    crossing, per fill   0.5 * 2.4110232735988832 * 0.50   = 0.6027558183997208
    commission, per fill 0.5 * 3.00                        = 1.50
    per fill                                               = 2.1027558183997208
    ROUND TRIP           = 2 * 2.1027558183997208          = 4.205511636799441

    the same sum written flat: 3.00 + 2.4110232735988832 * 0.50 = 4.205511636799441
    The two routes agree bit for bit (0x4010d271a47c010e), as §0's halving argument predicts.

    Rounded to the cent: round(4.205511636799441, 2) = 4.21 (no tie). 4.21 is
    0.0044883632005590 above the exact figure.


3. THE ONE-TICK RULE: a round trip is commission + one tick
================================================================================
Cost per side = comm/2 + 0.5 tick, so one round trip is exactly `comm_rt + tick_usd`. In the
vocabulary of §0 that is TickCrossing(1.0). Commission is $3.00 on a micro and $6.00 on a
full-size contract; the size charged is each root's minimum tradable size. Table line:
"one_tick".

    root  min_size  comm    tick_usd   ROUND TRIP = comm + tick_usd     printed to the cent
    NQ      MNQ     3.00      0.50      3.50                            3.50   exact
    ES      MES     3.00      1.25      4.25                            4.25   exact
    CL      MCL     3.00      1.00      4.00                            4.00   exact
    SI      SIL     3.00      5.00      8.00                            8.00   exact
    ZN      ZN      6.00     15.625    21.625                           21.63  ROUNDED
    ZB      ZB      6.00     31.25     37.25                            37.25  exact
    ZF      ZF      6.00      7.8125   13.8125                          13.81  ROUNDED

  ZN is a genuine tie. 21.625 is exactly representable (173/8), so rounding it to two
  decimals is a tie at the third digit:
      half-up   -> 21.63
      half-even -> 21.62     (what Python's round(21.625, 2) returns)
  The test asserts the exact 21.625 and records both roundings.
      13.8125 -> 13.81 under both rules (the third digit is 2, no tie).

  MNQ two ways:
      measured crossing   3.00 + 2.4110232735988832 * 0.50 = 4.205511636799441
      one-tick rule       3.00 + 1.0                * 0.50 = 3.50
  The rule is 0.7055116367994 cheaper. It is a convention, not a measurement.

================================================================================
MEASURED: a half-spread of 0.18219682151764224 bp a side on ES, at a median price of 6919.5,
converted to ticks on each contract (table line "effective_es_bp"):

    ES   crossing_ticks  = 1.0085687251930604
    MES  crossing_ticks  = 1.0085687251930602

  ONE ULP APART, AND THE TABLE KEEPS BOTH. The bp figure is one number; the tick figure is that

  divisions do not land on the same double. Each size entry carries the tick count of its own
  contract.

DECLARED commission: ES $4.00 / RT, MES $3.00 / RT.

  ES:
    crossing, per fill    0.5 * 1.0085687251930604 * 12.50 = 6.3035545324566275

5. FULL-SIZE COMMISSION HAS TWO DECLARED VALUES
================================================================================
  The table's `full` entries carry $6.00 / RT. Its `disagreements` block records an
  alternative $4.00 / RT assumption for ES, which is what §4 uses. Neither is measured.


6. CENT-QUOTED ROOTS
================================================================================
  data/fut_specs_from_definition.json computes a raw tick value as tick_price_units *
  unit_of_measure_qty, which is in dollars only where the price is quoted in dollars per unit.
  For the grains and livestock it is in cents. The file also carries `tick_usd_full_contract`,
  decided by a notional test on the observed price, and the cost table reads that field:

           ZC / ZS / ZW   tick_usd  12.5    raw 1250.0    divisor 100
           HE / LE        tick_usd  10.0    raw 1000.0    divisor 100
           ZL             tick_usd   6.0    raw  600.0    divisor 100
           SR3            tick_usd   6.25   raw    6.25   divisor   1

  HG is why the divisor cannot come from `unit_of_measure`: HG and ZL are both "LBS" and differ
  by exactly 100, HG quoted in dollars per pound and ZL in cents.

8. WHAT THIS FILE DOES NOT ESTABLISH
================================================================================
- No strategy return; nothing here reads a bar.
- Commission is declared. The table says `"measured": false` on every commission row.
- The crossing measurements are recent (2025-09 .. 2026-09). A tick is fixed in price terms,
  so a recent spread applied to an older, cheaper window is optimistic. The table carries each
  line's measurement window for that reason.
- A quoted spread and an effective crossing are different statistics (a floor versus what an
  aggressor paid). The table never averages them.
