Why They All Fragment

The hull was rejected for anchoring on ancient history. The next two by score do the opposite and fail for the opposite reason — they chop the series into pieces far too short to be trendlines. Same bars, same axes, your lines on top for scale.

Every construction that scores well produces roughly four times too many lines, each roughly five times too short. That is not a coincidence between two methods; it is what the score rewards. Per-bar gradient agreement is completely indifferent to whether a gradient comes from one 46-bar line or from five 9-bar ones.

It is the same root cause as the null failure. The metric measures the slope marginal, not the structure — so fragmenting is free, and a construction can win by producing the right spread of slopes without ever committing to a line.
