Trends in Their Own Windows

The bars are partitioned. A window opens where a trend starts and closes when its gradient changes, and each window's trend is fitted on its own bars alone. Real daily candles from the 1,573-name mining fixture, log price axis — so a constant-gradient line renders straight.

The rule — dynamic windows

the bars are PARTITIONED. each window carries its own trend.

  open a window at bar s, with no gradient yet

  accumulate the pivots confirmed inside it
      (a pivot at p is knowable at p + k, never before)

  once the window holds ≥ 5 pivots, fit G once
      — that is this window's gradient

  keep re-fitting g over [s, now] as the window grows
      when |g − G| > h the trend has CHANGED:
      close the window, open the next one here

  the line = tightest offset at slope G
             over the window's own bars

Every earlier version fitted the gradient over a fixed trailing 252 bars, so a single fit could straddle two regimes and describe neither. The previous fix reset the intercept's window at a re-anchor but left the gradient still reading a year back. Now the window boundary governs both: a retired trend contributes nothing to its successor.

And the cost is large, so look at it before accepting it. Each new window must re-earn its 5 pivots from scratch, and pivots arrive every 10–20 bars — so roughly 60–75% of every window has no gradient at all. State-on falls from ~100% to ~10%. The long gaps in the charts below are not missing data; they are the construction saying there is no trend it can justify yet.

The ≥5-pivot rule has gone from a no-op to the binding constraint. Under a fixed 252-bar lookback it voided nothing — the thinnest fit rested on 14 pivots, median 23. Under dynamic windows every window starts empty, so that same rule is now what decides when a trend may be declared at all, and it is the reason for the gaps.

The ≥5-consecutive-bar rule now bites too, for the first time: two bars dropped on F, one on WYNN. Short qualifying stretches are discarded rather than counted.
