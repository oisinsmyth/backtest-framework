The Ratcheted Trendline

A trendline whose gradient is sticky and whose intercept moves only when price breaks it. Drawn on real daily candles from the 1,573-name mining fixture, log price axis, so a constant-gradient line is a straight line.

The rule

hold G, the gradient. each bar:

    if |g_fresh − G| > h    RE-ANCHOR   G := g_fresh
                                       and the window resets to here

    the line is then the TIGHTEST offset at slope G
    that the bars since that re-anchor still respect:

        support     L = min over [anchor, now] of (low − G·i)
        resistance  L = max over [anchor, now] of (high − G·i)

a trend also requires  ≥ 5 pivots under the fit
                       ≥ 5 consecutive bars of state
                       otherwise: no trend here

The reset is back, and it was missing. Two versions ago the intercept could only loosen — MSFT ran 233 bars with no re-anchor and no push, drifting ~30% below the lows, and GME's resistance was dragged onto a spike high it could never come down from. Replacing the accumulator with a rolling minimum fixed that but threw away the reset: a re-anchor declared a new trend while lows from up to a year back, belonging to the old gradient, kept pinning the new line.

The window now starts at the re-anchor. A retired gradient stops voting. On the re-anchor bar itself the window is one bar, so each new trend is measured from its own beginning. No new parameter — the boundary is the construction's own event, capped at S2's 252-bar fit window so a gradient that never re-anchors cannot look back forever.

Both trend rules are non-binding on these six names. Over 47,220 fits the thinnest rests on 14 pivots (median 23, spanning a median 238 bars), so the ≥5-pivot rule voids nothing; and no state run was shorter than 5 bars, so the ≥5-run rule drops nothing. The gradient was never based on two bars. The defect was in the intercept.
