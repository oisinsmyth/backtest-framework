============================================================================
0. IS ES A LARGE-TICK BOOK?  (the load-bearing premise)
============================================================================
arXiv 2508.06788 Table 1: average spread p1..p75 = 0.25, p95 = 0.26,
p99 = 0.30, mean 0.25 sd 0.01.  The ES spread IS one tick, essentially
always. So the mid moves on queue depletion, in half-tick increments,
and a taker's round turn costs one FULL tick against the mid.

============================================================================
1. THE ONE-SECOND MOVE, IN TICKS AND DOLLARS
============================================================================
paper's era, index ~1250     1 tick = 2.000 bp | 1-sec SD 0.91 bp =  0.46 ticks = $  5.69
today, index ~6700           1 tick = 0.373 bp | 1-sec SD 0.91 bp =  2.44 ticks = $ 30.49

NOTE: holding SD_1S_BP fixed across eras assumes similar RELATIVE vol.
The 2008-2013 sample spans the GFC, so its 0.91bp is a HIGH-vol read;
carrying it to today therefore FAVOURS the strategy. Both are shown.

And the median one-second ES mid move is EXACTLY ZERO:
  Table 1 percentiles 25%/50%/75% of the 1-sec mid return = 0.00 bp.
  A majority of seconds contain no mid-price move to predict at all.

2. E[gross move | imbalance signal] vs THE ROUND TURN
============================================================================
E[r | s] = rho * sigma * s, with rho = sqrt(R2_OS).
R2 is imported from Nasdaq EQUITIES (Kolm et al.); no published ES
equivalent exists. ES is large-tick/deep-queue/high-update, which is
exactly the profile where Kolm finds the BEST R2 -- so the transfer
is GENEROUS to the strategy, and the conclusion survives it anyway.

============================================================================
2b. THE ONE CELL THAT CLEARS, STRESSED
============================================================================
Only R2=5% x 3sd x today's index clears ($20.45 vs $16.50). It stacks
five favourable choices. Two are checkable:

(i) VOLATILITY. 0.91 bp/sec is a 2008-2013 read spanning the GFC.
    Implied annualised vol = sd * sqrt(seconds per RTH year):
    0.91 bp/sec -> 22% ann. vol | sigma_1s $30.49 | best-cell E[gross] $20.45 vs cost $16.50 -> CLEARS
    0.75 bp/sec -> 18% ann. vol | sigma_1s $25.13 | best-cell E[gross] $16.85 vs cost $16.50 -> CLEARS
    0.62 bp/sec -> 15% ann. vol | sigma_1s $20.77 | best-cell E[gross] $13.93 vs cost $16.50 -> FAILS
    At anything below ~0.85 bp/sec -- i.e. below ~21%% annualised --
    the last surviving cell fails too.

(ii) LINEAR EXTRAPOLATION TO 3sd. E[r|s] = rho*sigma*s is linear and
     OVERSTATES at the extreme: Gould-Bonart's fitted relation
     SATURATES at P(up) ~ 0.8-0.9. Section 5 uses that measured
     conditional probability instead of extrapolating, and at the
     one-second horizon it loses in every cell. Where the two methods
     disagree, the saturating one is the correct one.

granularity: the mid moves in HALF ticks on a pinned book; a FULL tick
per mid-price change is carried as the generous alternative.

============================================================================

best large-tick R2_OS 5%     -> directional accuracy 0.572
Gould-Bonart 2016 out-of-sample AUC, large-tick: 0.752 - 0.805

6. MICROS ARE WORSE, NOT SAFER
============================================================================
ES : spread $12.50 + $4.00 = $16.50 = 1.32 ES ticks
MES: spread $1.25 + $1.00 = $2.25 = 1.80 MES ticks
MES: spread $1.25 + $1.50 = $2.75 = 2.20 MES ticks
Commission is a LARGER fraction of the micro tick, so sizing down
into MES raises the cost hurdle by 36-67%.
