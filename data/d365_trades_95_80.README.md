# d365_trades_95_80 — the momentum buffer book, every trade, every bar

Three files, written by `scripts/d365_export_trades.py --export`, all for D365's primary cell:
**enter above the 95th percentile of `mom_252_21`, hold until the 80th**, equal-weight,
hedged against the floored universe, next-open fill. Regenerate with the same command; the
numbers are asserted against the committed `data/d365_momentum_buffer.json` before a row is
written, so a stale file cannot exist quietly.

| file | rows | what one row is |
|---|--:|---|
| `d365_trades_95_80.csv` | 1,709 | one **trade** — a name, an entry bar, an exit bar |
| `d365_trade_paths_95_80.csv.gz` | 169,953 | one **bar of one trade** |
| `d365_series_95_80.csv` | 3,186 | one **bar of the book** |

**They reconcile, and that is asserted, not asserted-ish.** Every trade's bar returns sum to
its own P&L to 0.0, and the open trades' equal-weight mean equals the book's gross on every
one of the 3,186 deployed bars to 2.1e-17. Writing eight decimal places to CSV costs
1.6e-07 bp on the longest holds; that is the files' true precision.

## Sign and frame

All returns are **hedged** unless the column says otherwise: the name's return less the
floored universe's equal-weight return on the same bar. **Positive is money made.** The entry
bar earns open-to-close less the market's open-to-close (the D340 fill); every later bar
earns close-to-close less the market's. `pnl_unhedged_bp` is the name alone and
`pnl_market_bp` is what the hedge did, so `hedged = unhedged − market` bar by bar.

Prices are **as-traded** (`RAW_CLOSE`), not split-adjusted, so `price_close` is what the
screen showed that day.

## Which columns are lagged, and which are hindsight

**Lagged (t−1 information, safe to condition on):** every `pct_*` column, `mom_raw`, `dv`,
`dv_pct`, `beta`, both half-spreads. The percentile grids are D350's `lagged` closure, so a
value stamped at bar t was computable at the close of t−1. This is what R9 requires and it is
why these columns can be used to build a rule.

**Outcomes (not inputs):** `pnl_*`, `mfe_bp`, `mae_bp`, `bars_to_*`, `hold_bars`,
`exit_reason`, `pct_mom_exit`, `price_close_exit`.

**Hindsight, and marked as such:** `status_meta` and `delist_date` describe how the name
eventually ended. Split on them to understand the book; never select on them.

## Columns worth knowing about

- `exit_reason` — `rank` (fell below the 80th, 1,376 trades), `ineligible` (fell out of the
  floor or the deal filter, 260), `delist` (15), `open_at_end` (58, still held at the last
  bar and closed there).
- `mfe_bp` / `mae_bp` — the best and worst *cumulative* hedged P&L reached during the hold,
  with the bar each happened on. These are what an exit study reads.
- `two_c_PUB_bp` / `two_c_PB_bp` — D363's per-trade cost line: the name's own half-spread at
  its own entry **and** exit bar plus commission at its own as-traded price. `pnl_net_*` is
  the trade net of it.
- `members_at_entry` — how many names the book held that bar, so a trade can be weighted by
  the capital it actually got.

## The series file, and the one thing to know about its cost column

`cost_bp` is a **per-bar constant**: the book's round trip times its turnover, both
book-level quantities. It is the record's own arithmetic and it is not a per-bar cost
measurement, so do not read variation into it — there is none. `net_PUB_bp` is
`gross_bp − cost_bp`, and `equity_net_PUB_bp` is its running sum, which is what the drawdown
work in `--risk` reads.

Note two gross statistics exist and they differ by membership weighting: the **pooled** mean
over held name-bars (+3.37, the headline) and the **per-bar** series mean (+3.44, what
`gross_bp` in this file carries and what vol, Sharpe and drawdown are computed on).

## What this is not

Not a book entry, not an out-of-sample test, and not a recommendation. D365's cell was chosen
from an eleven-combination screen on this same fixture, so everything here is in-sample.
Anything found in these rows is a hypothesis for its own pre-registration.
