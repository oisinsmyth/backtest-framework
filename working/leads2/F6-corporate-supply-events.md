# F6 — corporate supply and float events: external evidence

*Commissioned 2026-09-09. Research brief, not a measurement. Under the quarantine contract in
[`README.md`](README.md) this closes nothing and admits nothing. Nothing here was run against the
fixture; every number below is somebody else's.*

---

## 1. Verdict

**Rank: (1) buyback EXECUTION, (2) IPO lockup expiries, (3) SEOs/secondaries, (4) spin-offs — and
the gap between 1 and 4 is not close.** Three of the four fail on the same wall, and it is not the
one the commission expected. It is not decay: it is that **the supply event resolves outside the
bar**. A buyback announcement, an SEO pricing and a spin-off distribution are all overnight events
whose entire documented payoff is in the open; on daily bars this programme is a bar late by
construction, and the literature's announcement CARs (buybacks: **2-day 1.7% average since 2000**,
down from 4.7% in 1999; SEOs: **−2% to −3% two-day**; spin-offs: **~3.0–3.6% announcement**) are
gone before the first tradeable print. **Spin-offs additionally fail the event-count gate outright**
— 15–25 US distributions a year, ~250–400 over 2010–2026 market-wide, of which a fixed 1,573-name
panel plausibly contains 50–150 — **and their headline mechanism has a direct peer-reviewed
refutation**: Abarbanell, Bushee & Raedy (*J. Business* 2003) tested exactly the "non-discretionary
holders are forced to sell" story and found that trading **"is not generally associated with
abnormal price movements for parents or subsidiaries around the spin-off."** The forced seller is
real; the price pressure is not. **The long-run drift for both repurchases and SEOs is documented
dead** in a top journal on the era we care about: Fu & Huang (*Management Science* 2016) find the
long-run abnormal returns following both **"disappear for the events in 2003–2012."** Lockups are
the cleanest calendar-dated supply shock in equities and I rank them second only because they are
structurally hostile to *this* book: the effect is **−1.5% over three days with more than half of it
landing on the unlock day itself** (Field & Hanka, 1,948 lockups, **1988–1997**), it is a **short**
in recent-IPO names — the hardest-to-borrow, lowest-priced tail this programme's $5 floor and
per-share commissions punish — and since ~2018 the single deterministic date has been engineered
away by staggered tranches and price-triggered early releases. **The one survivor is buyback
execution, and it survives for a reason none of the four were commissioned on**: it is not an event
at all, it is a **persistent state**. Rule 10b-18 puts a price-insensitive bid of up to 25% of ADTV
under a stock for months at a time, that bid is documented to concentrate **in down markets** and to
**provide liquidity when other investors sell** (Hillert–Maug–Obernberger, *JFE* 2016;
Busch–Obernberger, *RFS* 2017), the universe of repurchasing firms is exactly the profitable
higher-priced mid/large caps the cost regime tolerates, and the event count is tens of thousands of
firm-quarters. **It is invisible in real time — the SEC rule that would have made execution
observable daily was vacated (below) — but it does not need to be visible in real time if it is
persistent**, and persistence is a one-number Stage-0 measurement (§9).

### 2.2 Before or after — the question that was asked

**On the day, not before, and not reversed.** Field & Hanka's own decomposition puts **more than
half of a three-day −1.5% on the unlock day itself**, and the abnormal return "is not quickly
reversed." Volume, by contrast, moves *ahead* of the date and stays permanently higher. Gibbs & Hao
add the anticipation leg explicitly: **short interest builds before expiry and unwinds after**. So
the tape is: shorts accumulate through the run-up, the price takes a discrete hit on unlock day, and
the float is permanently larger afterwards.

**What that means for a daily-bar book:** the tradeable window is one bar, entered short at the prior
close. A single-bar −1.5% mean (−2.3% for the VC-backed subset) against a measured
**33.8 bp/side** spread on held names, plus borrow on a recent IPO, plus per-share commission on a
name that may be trading in single digits, is not obviously a positive-expectancy trade — and that
is the **1988–1997** magnitude.

### 2.3 Post-2010 survival — and the structural decay nobody mentions

**I could not find a single post-2010 US peer-reviewed replication.** The modern lockup literature I
reached is Nordic, Malaysian and Brazilian, and largely student theses. That absence is itself the
finding: **the era this fixture covers is the era nobody has published on.** [UNVERIFIED — treat as
a gap, not as evidence of decay.]

**But the decay mechanism is structural and documented by practitioners, not by returns.** The
single deterministic date has been engineered out of modern deals:

- **Staggered tranches.** Cooley's capital-markets group [PRACTITIONER, law firm — not peer-reviewed,
  but primary on deal terms]: Snowflake released 25% at day 91, DoorDash 40% at day 91, Braze 20% at
  day 50.
- **Price-conditioned early release.** "All of the most recent (2024) IPOs with staggered releases
  have included a performance condition," with thresholds **20–50% above the IPO price** releasing
  **10–35% of shares**.
- **Blackout pull-forward.** Lockups now commonly expire a fixed number of trading days *before* the
  earnings blackout starts — so the release date is keyed to an **earnings date**, which is itself a
  confound this programme has a separate brief on (F3).
- **Underwriter waivers.** A discretionary early release is frequently not filed at all.
- **Direct listings** generally have no lockup, so no event. **SPAC/de-SPAC lockups** are typically
  12 months with a **$12-for-20-of-30-days** early release, i.e. a *price-conditioned* date — which
  makes the event endogenous to the return you are trying to measure. That is a selection trap, not
  a data inconvenience.

**Net:** "IPO date + 180 calendar days" — the only rule you can compute cheaply — is, for a
meaningful fraction of the post-2018 sample, **the wrong date**, and wrong in a way correlated with
past returns.

### 2.4 Where it lives

Small, high-growth, VC/PE-backed, recently listed, frequently low-priced. **De-SPACs 2021–2022 lost
59–67% of value versus the $10 de-SPAC price**, which puts a large share of the modern lockup
population below or near this programme's **$5 floor** at the moment of the event — and per-share
commissions in bp scale inversely with price, so the cheapest names carry the worst cost. This is
the same tail that killed D284.

## 3. Buybacks

**Two objects. Keep them apart, because they fail differently.**

### 3.1 Announcement — documented, decayed, and gone before you can trade it

|---|---|---|---|
| Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen, *JFE* 1995 | [PEER-REVIEWED] | US OMR announcements 1980–1990 | **+12.1% four-year buy-and-hold abnormal return**; **+45.3% for value stocks, ~0 for glamour** |
| Mitchell & Stafford, *J. | MDPI *JRFM* 13(8):176, 2020 | [PEER-REVIEWED, low-tier — **single-sourced, verify before quoting**] | US OMR announcements | 2-day CAR: **4.7% (1999) → 0.4% (2006)**; **average 1.7% since 2000**; subsequent announcements by the same firm earn progressively less |

**Read Fu–Huang and Mitchell–Stafford together and the announcement drift is not a live anomaly for
2010–2026.** What remains is a ~1.7% two-day announcement pop, which is (a) a *news* effect, not
route (a), and (b) delivered in the open on a press release that lands before the bell or after the
close.

### 3.2 Execution — this is the actual route (a)

**The mechanism is bounded and documented.** Rule 10b-18 (17 CFR 240.10b-18) gives a safe harbour
only if the issuer's purchases satisfy, per day: **≤25% of ADTV**, a **single broker**, **not the
opening transaction and not in the final 10 (or 30) minutes**, and at a price **not exceeding the
highest independent bid or the last independent transaction price**. One block purchase per week may
substitute for the 25% cap. So the "price-insensitive buyer" is precisely characterised: **a
never-crossing-the-spread, up-to-a-quarter-of-volume, passive bid that cannot chase.** It absorbs
sellers; it does not initiate.

| source | tag | data | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ben-Rephael, Oded & Wohl, *Rev. Finance* 18(4):1299–1340, 2014 | [PEER-REVIEWED] | monthly actual repurchases from **10-Q Item 703** | firms repurchase at prices **significantly below average market prices**; discount **decreasing in size, increasing in market-to-book** |
| Dittmar & Field, *JFE* 2015 ("Can managers time the market?") | [PEER-REVIEWED, abstract only] | monthly repurchase price data | same direction: issuers buy below the period average |
| Hillert, Maug & Obernberger, *JFE* 119(1):186–209, 2016 | [PEER-REVIEWED] | **50,204 repurchase-months, US, 2004–2010** | repurchases **unequivocally improve liquidity** (IV); **"repurchases provide liquidity when other investors sell the firm's stock or in times of crisis"** |
| Busch & Obernberger, *RFS* 30(1):324–362, 2017 | [PEER-REVIEWED] | manually collected US actual repurchases | repurchases **improve price efficiency and reduce idiosyncratic risk**, **"driven by repurchases in down markets"**; **"price support at fundamental values"**; no evidence of manipulation |

**That is the literature saying, in four independent places, that the issuer is the counterparty who
shows up on down days.** For a programme whose stated problem is that "a reversal edge is
denominated in the spread," a documented partner that absorbs the same flow you would be absorbing —
without crossing — is the most direct hit in this brief.

**The catch is observability, and it is severe.** See §3.3. You cannot see today's issuer bid. You
can only see **last quarter's monthly totals, filed 40–45 days after that quarter ended**. The
signal is therefore not "the issuer is buying today" but "**this name is inside an active repurchase
program**" — a state, lagged by up to ~130 days at worst. Whether that state is a usable conditioner
is entirely a question of its persistence, which is §9.

### 3.3 The disclosure regime, with dates — **the commission's premise is wrong**

> The commission asked me to establish what "the SEC required since 2024." **It required nothing
> new. The rule was vacated before it produced usable data.**

| date | event |
|---|---|
| Dec 2021 | SEC **proposes** Form SR — daily repurchase detail, filed **one business day** after execution |
| **2023-05-03** | SEC **adopts** the Share Repurchase Disclosure Modernization rule (Rel. 34-97424). **Form SR is dropped for domestic issuers**; instead a **daily** repurchase table is filed as an **exhibit under Item 601(b)(26) of Reg S-K** to the **10-Q/10-K**, **tagged in Inline XBRL**, plus narrative rationale, a 10b5-1 checkbox, and Item 408(d) plan-adoption disclosure |
| 2023-06-01 | published in the Federal Register |
| **2023-10-31** | Fifth Circuit (*Chamber of Commerce v. SEC*) holds the SEC acted **arbitrarily and capriciously** under the APA — failed to respond to comments, failed on cost-benefit — and gives the SEC time to cure |
| **2023-10-01** | the compliance trigger that never bit: applicable to the first periodic report for **the first full fiscal quarter beginning on or after 2023-10-01** |
| **2023-12-19** | the SEC did not cure; the Fifth Circuit **VACATES the rule** |
| 2024-02-09 | SEC Corp Fin announcement: **"As a result of the vacatur, the disclosure requirements revert to those in effect prior to the Final Rule's effective date."** |
| **2024-04-08** | SEC **technical amendments** (Rel. **34-99778**) effective — the vacated text is removed from the CFR. **No re-proposal found as of this brief.** |

**Therefore, for the whole fixture 2010-01-04 → 2026-08-26, the governing regime is the
pre-existing Item 703 of Regulation S-K**, unchanged:

- **Where:** Form 10-Q Part II Item 2 (Q1–Q3) and Form 10-K Part II Item 5 (Q4). Foreign issuers:
  20-F/6-K.
- **What:** a table, **aggregated by MONTH**, for each class: **(a)** total shares purchased
  (*all* issuer repurchases, including those *not* under an announced plan), **(b)** average price
  paid per share, **(c)** shares purchased **under publicly announced plans or programs**,
  **(d)** maximum shares/dollars still available under those plans. Footnotes carry the **plan
  announcement date, size and expiry**.
- **Granularity:** monthly. **Daily is not available anywhere, for any US issuer, for any year in
  this fixture.** Only a handful of off-calendar-fiscal-year filers could have filed a daily table
  in Nov–Dec 2023 before the vacatur; that is not a dataset.
- **Machine-readable?** **No.** It is an HTML table in the filing body. The Inline XBRL tagging was
  in the vacated rule. **This is the single largest engineering cost in this territory.**
- **One-line cheat, with a contamination warning:** the XBRL element
  `PaymentsForRepurchaseOfCommonStock` from the cash-flow statement **is** tagged, quarterly, free,
  and available for every filer via `data.sec.gov` companyfacts and the Financial Statement Data
  Sets. It is orders of magnitude cheaper than parsing Item 703. **But it conflates open-market
  10b-18 buying with share retirements for tax withholding on RSU/option vesting**, which are not a
  bid in the market at all. Item 703 **column (c)** is the clean discriminator and exists only in
  the HTML. Use XBRL for the Stage-0 persistence screen; do not use it for a study.
  *(Adjacency flag: this is not the excluded "XBRL cash runway" construct — different element,
  different question — but it is the same pipe.)*

**One more regime change with a date:** the **1% excise tax on net share repurchases** (Inflation
Reduction Act) applies to repurchases **after 2022-12-31**, with a **netting rule** reducing the base
by stock *issued* in the same year. It changes the level of buyback activity and couples buybacks to
issuance within a firm-year. **Any pre-2023 / post-2023 comparison in this territory is confounded by
it.**

## 4. SEOs and secondaries

| source | tag | sample | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mikkelson & Partch 1986 | [PEER-REVIEWED, cited secondhand] | US primary offerings 1963–1981 | **two-day announcement CAR −3%** |
| Loughran & Ritter 1995/1997 | [PEER-REVIEWED] | US issuers | the **new-issues puzzle**: 5-year holding returns **33.4% for SEO issuers vs 92.8% for matched non-issuers**; no significant underperformance in the first 6 months, then ~18 months of it |
| Spiess & Affleck-Graves | [PEER-REVIEWED, cited secondhand] | US SEOs | median 5-yr **10% vs 42.3%** for size-matched non-issuers |
| Corwin, *JF* 2003 | [PEER-REVIEWED] | US SEOs | **offer priced at ~3% discount to the prior close**; underpricing **increased over time**; **low-priced securities are more underpriced** (offer prices round to even dollars); price drifts down from announcement to offer |
| Mola & Loughran 2003 | [PEER-REVIEWED, cited secondhand] | US SEOs, 2000s | mean initial return **2.83%** |
| Mitchell & Stafford 2000 | [PEER-REVIEWED] | equity issues among three event families | long-run underperformance **not reliable** after cross-correlation correction; what survives is small stocks |
| **Fu & Huang, *Man. Sci.* 2016** | [PEER-REVIEWED] | US SEOs | **long-run abnormal returns disappear for 2003–2012** |

**Where the payoff actually is, and why it is not ours.** The reliably-measured money in an SEO is
the **~3% offer discount**, and that is a *primary-market allocation*, paid to the institutions the
underwriter allocates to. An open-market participant on daily bars sees: an announcement after the
close, a book built and priced overnight, and an opening print the next morning that already
contains the discount. Corwin's own finding that **the discount is largest for low-priced
securities** points the residual straight into the sub-$10 tail where per-share commissions bite
hardest.

**Direction is also ambiguous in exactly the wrong way.** The *public* offering announcement earns
a negative CAR; a *private* placement announcement is reported at **+4.5%** [cited secondhand,
unverified]. Sponsor block sales (424B7 resales) are a different animal again from a
capital-raising primary. **These are not one event type**, and a study that pools them is measuring
a mixture whose sign is a composition effect.

**Boundary with the excluded ATM territory.** Both a marketed follow-on and an ATM programme file a
**424B5**. The discriminator is inside the document: an ATM supplement describes an "at the market
offering" under Rule 415(a)(4) with a **sales agent** and **no fixed offer price**; a single priced
deal carries a **fixed public offering price, named underwriters, and a settlement date**, and is
normally accompanied by an **8-K Item 1.01** (underwriting agreement). **If a study cannot make that
split reliably it is not studying SEOs, it is studying the excluded territory.**

### 5.2 THE WARNING — what a spin-off does to a price series

**This section is worth more than the signal, and it generalises past spin-offs.**

A spin-off is a **distribution in kind**: economically a special dividend whose per-share value is
not known until the child trades. Four separate things break, and the programme's own memory note
(*fixtures disagree on corporate-action basis — 15m is fully adjusted, daily is not; NOW sat at 5×
its own prices and `raw_price_factor` cannot fix a spinoff*) is a live instance of the third.

**(1) The parent gaps down at the ex-distribution date, and the gap is not a return.** On an
unadjusted daily close series the ex-date shows as a large negative one-bar return with no news.
**A reversal book reads that as a crash and buys it.** Every spin-off parent in a non-adjusted daily
fixture carries one fabricated crash. So does every large special cash dividend.

**(2) The correct adjustment for the parent** is a multiplicative factor applied to **every price
strictly before the ex-date**:

```
f = ( P_parent_cum  -  r * P_child ) / P_parent_cum
```

where `r` = child shares received per parent share, `P_child` = the child's **when-issued close on
the last trading day before ex** (index methodologies use exactly this; if no when-issued market
exists, the child's first regular-way price, and some index families **skip the adjustment entirely**
when there is no WI market), and `P_parent_cum` = the parent's last cum-distribution close. This is a
**step in a cumulative multiplicative factor**, identical in kind to a cash special dividend. **It is
NOT a share-count change** — CRSP is explicit that for spin-offs the price factor and the
shares-outstanding factor are different objects and the shares factor is set to zero.

**(3) The child has NO price history before its when-issued / first regular-way date.** Any earlier
bar for the child is either fabricated or is the parent's history wearing the child's ticker. **This
is the 5× failure mode.** Multiple vendors record the spin-off continuity factor **in the same
split-history table as ordinary splits**, as ratios like `2000:1973` or `5000:4912`. Anything
downstream that reads that table as split ratios — or that reads a **distribution ratio** (1 child
per 5 parent) as a **5-for-1 split** — shifts the entire pre-event series by a clean integer factor.
**A single scalar `raw_price_factor` cannot repair it**, for two independent reasons: the error is
**date-dependent** (a step at one bar, not a constant scale), and in the child's case it is an
**absence of data**, not a mis-scaling of data. There is nothing to multiply.

**(4) Identifier reuse.** The parent often changes ticker at the spin, or the child takes the
parent's historic name. **Key on CIK / PERMNO, never on ticker**, across the distribution date.

**Assertions worth carrying whether or not a spin-off study is ever written** (in the house style of
"break what the assertion reads"):

- For every known distribution: assert the parent's **total** return on the ex-date
  (price change **plus** `r * P_child`) is within a few sigma of its own vol. A raw price-only return
  will fail this; that is the point.
- Assert the child's **first bar in the fixture is its first bar in existence** — i.e. no bar with a
  timestamp before the WI/first regular-way date. Break the check by prepending one parent bar and
  confirm it raises.
- Universal cheap screen, no event list required: flag any bar with `|r| > 40%` where the following
  bar has `|r| < 5%` and the name's own vol does not support it, then look for a `10-12B`, an
  `8-K` in the window, or a large `DividendsCommonStock` XBRL fact. **Name the top offender and look
  at it.**

## 7. DATA — forms, cost, dead names, look-ahead

**All of the below is free, all of it is on EDGAR, and EDGAR is dead-inclusive by construction:
filings persist forever keyed on CIK, regardless of delisting or bankruptcy.** The hard part is
never the filing — it is **CIK ↔ ticker for names that no longer exist**. `company_tickers.json`
covers current registrants only. `Form 25` / `25-NSE` (Notification of Removal from Listing, filed on
EDGAR since **2006-04-24**, **delisting effective 10 days after filing**, deregistration at 90 days)
gives the delisting date but not the historic symbol. **Two clean point-in-time ticker sources exist
in this territory:** the IPO prospectus states the proposed symbol, and the spin-off Form 10 does the
same for the child.

| event | primary form(s) | carries what | free | dead names | look-ahead rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **IPO / lockup** | **S-1**, **S-1/A**, final prospectus **424B4** (or 424B1) | lockup **length, covered holders, early-release and staggered terms** — in "Shares Eligible for Future Sale" and "Underwriting". **Free text; no tag, no field** | yes | yes | **No look-ahead risk on the date itself** — the 424B4 filing date ≈ pricing date and the expiry is derivable from it at t0. That is precisely why the event is anticipated. **The risk is the opposite one: assuming "+180 days" when the deal had tranches or a price trigger.** Waivers and discretionary early releases may appear in an **8-K Item 8.01** or **nowhere at all** |
| **Buyback announcement** | **8-K Item 8.01** (Other Events) or **Item 7.01** (Reg FD), press release as **EX-99.1** | authorised size, term | yes | yes | **There is no mandatory form.** Absence of an 8-K is NOT absence of an announcement — many are inside the earnings release, and some appear first in a 10-Q. **The reliable source of announcement dates is the FOOTNOTE to the Item 703 table**, which states plan announcement date, size and expiry — and it is retrospective, so it is look-ahead-safe by construction |
| **Buyback execution** | **10-Q Part II Item 2** / **10-K Part II Item 5**, table per **Item 703 Reg S-K**; also XBRL `PaymentsForRepurchaseOfCommonStock` (contaminated — see §3.3) | **monthly** totals, avg price, plan-vs-non-plan split, remaining authorisation | yes | yes | **Deadline: 40 days after quarter end (large accelerated / accelerated), 45 days (non-accelerated).** So a March repurchase is public in early May at the earliest — a **40–130 day** lag depending on which month of the quarter. **Use `acceptanceDatetime`, not `filingDate`** (see below) and enter no earlier than the following bar |
| **SEO / secondary** | shelf **S-3 / S-3ASR**; takedown **424B5**; selling-shareholder resale **424B7**; **8-K Item 1.01** (underwriting agreement) and often **8.01** | offer price, size, underwriters, selling holders | yes | yes | **The announcement is after the close and the book prices overnight.** The 424B5 hits EDGAR that evening or the next morning; the first daily bar you can act on already contains the effect. Also: **424B5 is shared with the excluded ATM territory** — split on fixed-price-plus-underwriters vs Rule 415(a)(4) sales agent |
| **Spin-off** | child **Form 10-12B** + **10-12B/A**, Information Statement as **EX-99.1**; child **8-A12B** for listing; parent **8-K Item 8.01** announcing record date and ratio; completion **8-K Item 2.01/8.01** | **distribution ratio, record date, distribution date** — the three numbers the price adjustment needs | yes | yes | Record date and ratio are typically announced **2–4 weeks ahead** in an 8-K. **The supply event is the ex-distribution date, not the announcement** |

**Two mechanical rules that apply to all four.**

1. **`acceptanceDatetime`, never `filingDate`.** EDGAR assigns **that day's** filing date to a live
   submission begun **at or before 17:30 ET**; a submission begun after 17:30 gets a filing date of
   **06:00 the next business day**. (Exceptions taking the actual date up to 22:00: Forms 3/4/5,
   Rule 462(b) filings, and correspondence.) **So `filingDate` is neither the moment the document
   became public nor a bar boundary.** Use the acceptance timestamp from the filing index /
   `data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json` and require the next regular-hours bar.
2. **Getting the filings.** The existing `d331_edgar_deals.py` puller extends by **configuration**
   for `10-12B`, `424B4`, `424B5`, `424B7`, `25-NSE`, `S-1`. For **8-Ks the form type alone is
   useless** — the volume is enormous and the content is unstructured. Two routes:
   **(i) EDGAR full-text search** (`efts.sec.gov`), which indexes **all filings and exhibits from
   2001-05-04 to present**, all form types, free, with form-type and date filters, at the SEC's
   **10 req/s** ceiling — the programme's 8 req/s already complies;
   **(ii) the 8-K item codes carried in the submissions JSON / filing header**, to pre-filter to
   Items 8.01 / 7.01 / 2.01 / 1.01 before any text work. **[UNVERIFIED — I did not confirm the
   `items` field live; check it before planning around it.]**

## 8. Does any of it transfer to OUR universe — bluntly

**No, three times, and once with a real chance.**

- **The $5 floor and per-share commissions point the wrong way for three of the four.** Lockup
  expiries live in recent IPOs and de-SPACs — the population that lost **59–67%** off a $10 base and
  sits at or under the floor at exactly the moment of the event. SEO discounts are, by Corwin's own
  result, **largest in low-priced securities**. Mitchell & Stafford's surviving long-run effect is
  **"largely limited to small stocks."** This is the D284 shape, again. **Buyback execution is the
  single exception and it is a big one: repurchasing firms are profitable, higher-priced mid and
  large caps.** For once the cost regime and the population agree.
- **Equal weight has no purchase on 20 names a year.** Every construction here is equal-weighted and
  the book is a cross-section. Spin-offs cannot fill it. Lockups can only fill it in the IPO-heavy
  years (2020–2021), which means the study's exposure is concentrated in one regime — and that
  regime is the one whose names then fell 60%.
- **The overnight problem is structural, not fixable.** Buyback announcements, SEO pricings and
  spin-off announcements all resolve outside RTH. On daily bars the programme's first action is the
  next open. This is the same wall F3 will hit from the other side. **Buyback execution is again the
  exception, because it is not an announcement — it is a condition that persists across many bars.**
- **The short side is worse than it looks.** The lockup trade is a short in a recent IPO. Borrow on
  those names is the most expensive and least available in the market. The programme has already
  covered borrow and knows what that costs.
- **Two things transfer regardless of whether any study is written.** First, **the spin-off /
  special-dividend gap in a non-adjusted daily fixture is a live data defect right now** (§5.2), and
  the assertions in §5.2 cost nothing. Second, **the Item 703 footnote is a free, retrospective,
  look-ahead-safe source of buyback announcement dates** that nobody has to hunt 8-Ks for.

## 9. The premise number to compute first, for the best of the four

**Territory: buyback execution. The premise is persistence, and the Stage-0 gate is not a return.**

The mechanism (a 10b-18 bid, ≤25% of ADTV, non-crossing, concentrated in down markets) is only
tradeable if **last quarter's disclosed execution predicts this quarter's presence**, because that is
the only thing the disclosure regime lets you observe. If repurchase activity is a one-quarter burst,
the lagged flag is noise and there is no study. If programs run for quarters, the flag is a valid,
look-ahead-free conditioner on a book this programme already runs.

**Compute, on the fixture's own names, with the XBRL element as the cheap first pass
(§3.3 contamination caveat carried explicitly):**

1. **`P(repurchase > 0 in quarter q+1 | repurchase > 0 in quarter q)`**, and the same at q+2 and q+4,
   against the unconditional base rate `P(repurchase > 0)`. **The number that decides it is the
   lift over the base rate, not the level.** If a name that repurchased last quarter is barely more
   likely than average to repurchase this quarter, stop here.
2. **Coverage: the fraction of universe name-bars carrying an active flag**, using the
   look-ahead-safe rule (flag turns on at the 10-Q acceptance timestamp + 1 bar, and stays on until
   the next 10-Q says otherwise). **If it flags 60% of bars it is not a conditioner, it is a market
   proxy.** If it flags 3% the cross-section is too thin to be equal-weighted.
3. **Composition of the flagged set against what the cost regime cares about**: the price
   distribution (how much sits above $10, where per-share commission stops dominating), the
   dollar-volume distribution, the **dead-vs-alive split** (buybacks should tilt alive — confirm it,
   because if the flag is a survivorship proxy every downstream result is that and nothing else),
   and the **era split** across the 2023 excise-tax break.
4. **Magnitude sanity, one number:** median disclosed repurchase dollars per quarter as a fraction of
   the name's median quarterly dollar volume. **This bounds the mechanism.** If the issuer's whole
   quarterly buying is 0.3% of volume traded, a 25% ADTV cap on a handful of days is not a bid the
   book can lean on, and the RFS/JFE liquidity results will not scale down to it.

**Only if (1) shows real lift, (2) lands in a workable coverage band, and (4) is not negligible is
there a study — and it is not an event study.** It is a **conditioner on the existing reversal
book**: the hypothesis, in the programme's own framing, is that a name being absorbed by its own
issuer on down days is a name where the reversal edge is not entirely eaten by the toll. That is
route (a) in the form the disclosure regime actually permits.

**Second priority, and only after that:** count the fixture's IPO-lockup events for real (§6). The
whole lockup question is downstream of one number nobody has — **how many names in a fixed
1,573-name panel actually listed inside 2010–2026 and survived into it.** If that count is under
about 150, §2 is moot and the answer is "not worth writing."
