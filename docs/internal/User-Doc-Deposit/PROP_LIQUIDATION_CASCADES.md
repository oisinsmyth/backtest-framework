# Prop-Firm Liquidation Cascades: Study Design

**Status: KILLED AT PREMISE CHECK — 18 September 2026. Retained as a record.**

**Instruments (as originally proposed):** MES, MNQ, MCL, MGC
**Horizon:** minutes; flat by default

---

## 0. Why this study is dead

The mechanism required prop-firm traders' forced liquidations to reach the CME order book. **They
do not.** The premise was checked after the design was written and it is false:

- The overwhelming majority of funded prop accounts are **simulated**. Trading takes place in a
  simulated environment on real market data; payouts are made from the firm's reserves, funded
  mainly by evaluation fees.
- Topstep's 2025 disclosure: **0.71%** of participants in Express Funded Accounts were called up
  to a Live Funded Account. Apex keeps funded ("PA") accounts on sim indefinitely, moving only a
  hand-picked subset to live.
- The firm is the counterparty to its own traders. A book that is structurally profitable for the
  house has no reason to be hedged in the real market.

Consequently the population is large and the constraint is shared, **but the contracts are not
real.** Forced exits under the daily loss limit produce no aggressive volume in MNQ. The
signature this study was built to detect — micro-heavy flow not mirrored in the full-size
contract — is not generated by this population.

**This is the process working.** A premise check cost a day; building on it would have cost
months. The failure is recorded here rather than deleted because a study killed for a stated,
verifiable reason is a better artefact than one that was never checked.

**Lesson for the other studies:** any mechanism resting on "who trades the micros" must be
checked against *who actually routes orders to the exchange*, not who appears to be trading.

## 0.1 What survives, narrower

| Population | Reaches the book? | Constraint | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prop-sim traders | **No** | Uniform DLL | Dead |
| Prop **live**-funded tier | Yes | Uniform DLL | ~1% of the population; too small to move a book |
| Firms that **mirror** sim to live | Yes | Uniform DLL | At least one firm does this; verify scale |
| Firms hedging **net** sim exposure | Yes, via the firm's desk | Aggregate | **No evidence any do.** Apex's model suggests they do not |
| **Retail on real broker accounts** (Tradovate, NinjaTrader Brokerage, AMP, etc.) | **Yes** | Heterogeneous: own stops, broker margin | **Real, but diffuse** |

The last row is the only viable remnant. Retail stop-losses cluster at technical levels — round
numbers, prior-day extremes, opening-range boundaries — rather than at a firm-mandated
percentage. That is a forced-exit mechanism, but a **diffuse** one: no shared trigger, no sharp
cascade, and it is closer to the well-documented stop-hunting literature than to anything new.

If pursued, it is a **different, smaller, more crowded study**: "retail stop-clustering in the
micro book," using `MICRO_FLOW_SIGNAL.md` F2 bursts near technical levels as the detector. It
should not inherit this document's novelty claims or its Sharpe priors.

## Original design (retained for record)

## 1. The mechanism

### 1.1 The constrained population

Prop firms now fund tens of thousands of traders. The population has four properties that
matter, all of which follow from the firms' business model rather than from trader behaviour:

| Property | Why it holds |
|---|---|
| **Identical constraints** | Every trader at a firm is on the same daily loss limit and trailing drawdown, expressed as a percentage of nominal |
| **Same instruments** | Firms permit a short list — MES, MNQ, MCL, MGC dominate |
| **Correlated positioning** | Retail education converges on the same breakout / opening-range setups on the same 5-minute charts |
| **Forced exit** | Hitting the daily loss limit flattens the account mechanically, not at the trader's discretion |

### 1.2 The cascade

When the market moves against the crowd, traders who entered at similar levels with similar
loss limits **hit those limits at similar prices**. Each forced flat is an aggressive market
order. The orders arrive in a burst in the micros, overshoot the move, and then — with the
forced sellers gone — the price reverts.

This is a **margin-call cascade at retail scale**, and it passes the mechanism filter cleanly:
a named group, a published constraint (the firm's own rulebook), a derivable trigger, and a
price-insensitive forced trade.

### 1.3 Why it is observable

Institutions do not trade micros in size. A burst of aggressive volume in **MNQ that is not
mirrored in NQ** is retail and prop flow by construction. The micro/full-size divergence is the
signature, and it is directly visible in the order book data already held.

### 1.4 Why the edge should persist

- **Capacity** is tiny. The whole phenomenon lives in micro contracts.
- **Novelty.** Three to five years of history at most.
- **Nobody with a research budget cares.** The reversion after a $200k cascade is invisible to a
  fund.
- **The constraint is not going away** — it is the prop firms' risk-management model.

## 2. Derivation of the trigger

The forced-exit level for a trader is:

```
exit_price = entry_price  −  (DLL_pct × nominal) / (position_size × point_value)
```

You do not observe individual entries. But the population's entries **cluster** at identifiable
levels — opening range breaks, prior-day highs and lows, round numbers — and the DLL percentage
is published. So the **distribution of forced-exit levels** is estimable from:

1. Where aggressive micro buying clustered earlier in the session (proxy for crowd entries)
2. Typical position sizing under the firm's rules (bounded by margin and DLL)
3. The DLL percentage itself

This gives a **predicted cascade zone** below (or above) the crowd's entry cluster. That is the
derivable part. What is not derivable is *whether* price reaches it.

**So this is a conditional strategy:** if price enters the predicted zone, expect a cascade;
detect it in the flow; fade the overshoot.

## 3. Features

| ID | Feature | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Micro/full aggressive-volume ratio** | Signed aggressive volume in MNQ ÷ same in NQ, over a rolling 1–5 min window, z-scored against its own time-of-day history |
| F2 | **Cascade burst detector** | F1 exceeding a pre-registered threshold *while* price is moving against the prior micro flow direction |
| F3 | **Crowd entry cluster** | Volume-weighted price of aggressive micro buying (selling) over the prior N minutes — the estimated crowd entry |
| F4 | **Predicted cascade zone** | F3 offset by the DLL-implied distance (§2) |
| F5 | **Zone proximity** | Current price distance to F4, in ATR units |
| F6 | **Post-burst exhaustion** | Decline in F1 following a burst — forced sellers finished |

**Signal:** enter against the cascade direction when F2 fires **and** F6 confirms exhaustion.
Size by F1 magnitude. Exit on time (pre-registered horizon) or on F1 reverting to neutral.

**The order matters.** Entering on F2 alone is catching a falling knife. F6 is the piece that
distinguishes "cascade in progress" from "cascade finished," and it is where the edge is.

---

## 4. The critical discriminator

**The reversion after any sharp move is already collected by ordinary liquidity providers.**
This study has an edge only if the *micro-driven* cascade reverts more, or more reliably, than a
generic shock.

**Pre-registered test:** compare post-shock reversion on

- shocks where F1 is elevated (micro-heavy — the prop crowd)
- shocks of equal magnitude where F1 is normal (institution-driven)

**Prediction:** reversion is larger and more reliable in the first group, because the forced
sellers are uninformed and finite. **If there is no difference, the mechanism is not
operating** and the study stops.

| Data | Use | Status |

**Sample:** micros from 2019, but the prop population only became large post-2021.
**Effective clean sample: ~2021 onward, four to five years.** State this plainly. It is short,
and it is the single largest weakness of the study.

## 6. Targets

| Target | Window | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Entry → +5 min | Immediate reversion |
| R2 | Entry → +15 min | Primary |
| R3 | Entry → +30 min | Persistence |

Computed from tradeable prices with a crossing-the-spread assumption. Report IC decay across all
three; read the holding period off the curve.

---

## 7. Cost — the gate

Micro spreads are the same tick as full-size; commissions as a percentage of notional are
higher. Cascade entries are by definition into fast markets where the spread widens.

**Gate before any modelling:** crude first pass on F2 → R2, cost from the actual book at the
moment of entry. If the top-decile reversion is within 3× realistic all-in cost, stop.

---

## 8. Trial budget

**7 trials.** F1–F6 as features (6), plus the §4 discriminator test (1). Thresholds for F2 are
set from the time-of-day distribution (e.g. 99th percentile) and **not swept**.

---

## 9. Kill criteria

1. Cost gate fails (§7). Terminal.
2. §4 discriminator shows no difference between micro-heavy and institution-driven shocks.
   **Terminal — mechanism absent.**
3. Effect present only in 2021–22 (the peak retail-frenzy period) and gone after.
4. F1 bursts are not more frequent near F4 zones than elsewhere — the derivation is wrong.

---

## 10. Constraint fit

| Constraint | Fit |
|---|---|
| Futures only | Yes |
| Prop hedging rules | Directional, single position, no spreads |
| Trailing DD | Flat by default; minutes of exposure |
| Minimum activity | Several events per week across four instruments |
| Algorithmic | Fully — burst detection and timed exits |

**One structural irony worth stating in the writeup:** you will be trading against traders
operating under exactly the constraints you operate under. The DLL that forces their exit is the
same rule that governs your account. Knowing the constraint from the inside is a legitimate
informational advantage, and it is not one a fund researcher has.

---

## 11. Sequence

1. Build F1 across all four micro/full pairs; characterise its time-of-day distribution.
2. Cost gate on the crudest version of F2 → R2. **Stop if it fails.**
3. Build F3–F6.
4. Run the §4 discriminator. **Stop if no difference.**
5. Full harness, IC decay, CPCV (sessions as groups, embargo 3 sessions).
6. Combine with `OVERNIGHT_IMBALANCE.md` per `EVENT_PORTFOLIO.md`.

---

## 12. Open questions

1. Does the population size vary enough across firms and time to condition on?
2. Do the four instruments cascade **together** (crowd is in MNQ and MES simultaneously) or
   independently? Affects both breadth and risk.
3. Is there a **time-of-day structure** — cascades clustering in the first hour after the US
   open, when the opening-range crowd is most exposed?
4. Does the strategy's own execution affect the cascade at any realistic size? Almost certainly
   not at micro scale, but state it.

---

## 13. Honest position (original, superseded by §0)

The original assessment called this the best-motivated novel idea in the programme. It was, on
the information used to write it. It was wrong on the one fact that mattered — whether the
population's orders reach the exchange — and that fact was checkable in ten minutes.

The corrected position: the mechanism does not exist at detectable scale in the real book. The
narrower retail-stop remnant (§0.1) may be worth a small, low-expectation study later. Nothing
from this document's Sharpe priors or novelty claims should carry forward.
