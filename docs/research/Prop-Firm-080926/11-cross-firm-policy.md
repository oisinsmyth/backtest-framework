**Read the second column, not the first.** Four of the five never mention a competing firm at all,

### The sharp risk, answered

The brief asked whether a long at firm A against a short at firm B could be construed as hedging,
and whether any firm's hedging language is scoped to its own accounts or written broadly.

**Three tiers, and they are not the tiers you would guess:**

1. **FTMO — written broadly, deliberately, and by name.** Forbidden Trading Practice #2 prohibits
   entering opposite positions *"between connected accounts, accounts held with various
   operators/providers, or accounts held with other members of the Program Group"*. **"Various
   operators/providers" is the competing-firm slot, and FTMO filled it.** This is the only
   unambiguous cross-firm hedging prohibition among the five.
2. **Take Profit Trader — written broadly, probably by accident.** Rule 6 opens *"Take Profit Trader
   strictly prohibits holding opposite positions in the same or closely related products **across any
   accounts under the same beneficial control**."* No venue qualifier appears in that sentence. An ES
   long at TPT against an MES short at Apex satisfies it on its face.
3. **Topstep, MyFundedFutures, Apex — scoped to their own accounts in the operative document.**
   Topstep's Terms of Use §27 says *"different **Topstep** Parties"*, and "Topstep Parties" is a
   defined term meaning Topstep's own affiliates. MFFU's §23.1.3.3 sits inside a subsection framed
   entirely by *"the Company's Account Limits"*. Apex's rule governs *"All Apex accounts"*.

**But Topstep carries a documented discrepancy that cuts the other way.** Its help-centre article
*Understanding Hedging* quotes what it calls "CME Group Rule 534" as:

> *"Performing, alone or in concert with any other persons, including between connected accounts, or
> accounts held with **different Parties**, trades, or combinations of trades, the purpose of which is
> to manipulate, abuse, or give User an unfair advantage…"*

That string is **word-for-word Topstep's own Terms of Use §27 bullet, with the word "Topstep"
deleted** — and the deleted word is exactly the scoping word. Full comparison in §3.2. On the same
page: *"Confirmed hedging violations are final and cannot be appealed"* and *"Accounts closed due to
these violations cannot be reopened or reinstated."* **The document a trader is most likely to read
is broader than the contract that binds them, and the penalty it announces is terminal.**

### Disclosure — the flat answer

**No firm of the five requires a trader to disclose being funded elsewhere.** Not at registration,
not at KYC, not in any terms document, not in any help article read. TPT's Registration Process and
KYC Procedure articles collect identity only. The single cross-firm question asked anywhere is
Topstep's **negative warranty about debt**, not about funding (§3.2, quote T4).

### What the discretionary clauses do to all of the above

**"Unaddressed" does not mean "permitted".** Each firm reserves the right to end the relationship on
grounds broad enough to swallow the silence:

| firm | the clause that makes the written permission moot |
|---|---|
| **MFFU** | §17.1 — *"at any time and for any reason"*; §23.1.11 — anything the Company deems *"unethical, deceptive, manipulative, high-risk"* in its *"sole and reasonable discretion"* |
| **Topstep** | §21 — *"any or no reason"*; §24 — conduct that *"MAY CAUSE TOPSTEP RISK OF OR ACTUAL REPUTATIONAL DAMAGE"*; §27's final bullet — *"uncommercial activity… not a viable trading strategy"*, sole discretion |
| **Apex** | *"use the Company Services abusively or in a way that tries to exploit, 'game,' or undermine the purpose or spirit of the Program"*; *"Unsustainable Strategies"* |
| **TPT** | Sustainable Trading Policy — *"TPT reserves the right to step in at its sole discretion, with or without prior notice"*; *"TPT may part ways as funding partners without prior notice"* |
| **FTMO** | 7.3 — *"We reserve the right to determine, at our own discretion, whether certain… strategies, or situations qualify as or fall within a Forbidden Trading Practice"*; **13.2.4 — either party may terminate *"without cause"* on five business days' notice** |

**So the honest statement is: at four of five firms a multi-firm book is un-prohibited and
un-protected.** The only firm that engaged the question in writing (FTMO) engaged it to prohibit the
two-sided case.

## 2. The industry template, and where each firm set the scope dial

This is the load-bearing structural finding. **FTMO's Forbidden Trading Practice #2 and Topstep's
Terms of Use §27 bullet 5 are the same boilerplate sentence** — same opening verb, same "alone or in
concert", same "connected accounts", same "combinations of trades", same "manipulate, abuse, or give
User an unfair advantage", same closing "simultaneously entering into opposite positions". They
differ in **one slot**: what sits between "connected accounts" and the end of the enumeration.

| firm | the slot | scope achieved |
|---|---|---|
| **FTMO** | *"accounts held with **various operators/providers**, or accounts held with other members of the Program Group"* | **the whole industry** |
| **Topstep (ToU §27)** | *"accounts held with **different Topstep Parties**"* | **Topstep's own affiliates** |
| **Topstep (help centre)** | *"accounts held with **different Parties**"* | **ambiguous — reads as the whole industry** |

**The template has a cross-provider slot in it.** FTMO filled it; Topstep narrowed it in the
contract and then published the unnarrowed form in the article traders actually read. MFFU, Apex and
TPT use different drafting lineages and never enumerate venues at all.

#### The help-centre / contract discrepancy

**Source: [Understanding
Hedging](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/13747047-understanding-hedging), article dated **July
27, 2026**, read 2026-09-08.**

**T7 — what the article publishes, attributed to "CME Group Rule 534":**

> *"Performing, alone or in concert with any other persons, including between connected accounts, or
> accounts held with **different Parties**, trades, or combinations of trades, the purpose of which is
> to manipulate, abuse, or give User an unfair advantage while using the Site or Services, for
> example, by engaging in any short term or high-frequency trades or simultaneously entering into
> opposite positions"*

**Character-level diff against T1, Topstep's own Terms of Use §27:**

| | T1 (Terms of Use) | T7 (help centre, attributed to CME) |
|---|---|---|
| scoping phrase | *"different **Topstep** Parties"* | *"different Parties"* |
| hyphenation | *"high frequency"* | *"high-frequency"* |
| everything else | — | **identical, word for word** |

**Two things follow, and both matter.**

1. **The attribution is wrong in form.** The passage says *"while using the Site or Services"* and
   uses the defined term *"User"* — contract language from Topstep's own document, not exchange
   rulebook language. **I did not fetch the CME rulebook, so I do not assert what CME Rule 534
   contains; I assert only that this string is Topstep's §27 minus one word.** (TPT's Rule 6 article,
   independently, describes CME 534 as the **wash-trade** rule — consistent with the attribution
   being misplaced, but that is a prop firm citing an exchange and is not primary.)
2. **The deleted word is the scoping word.** A trader reading only the help centre would reasonably
   conclude that opposite positions across accounts *at different firms* are prohibited. **A trader
   reading the contract would not.** The contract is the instrument that binds; the article is what
   gets read.

**T8 — what the article scopes the policy to, in its own words:**

> *"Monitoring applies **in real-time**"* · *"Violations apply **across all accounts involved in
> hedging**"* · *"This policy applies to the **Trading Combine, Express, and Live Accounts**"*

**All three Topstep products. No mention of any account anywhere else.**

**T9 — the terminal penalty:**

> *"Confirmed hedging violations are final and cannot be appealed. Accounts closed due to these
> violations cannot be reopened or reinstated. We encourage you to review our trading policies to
> ensure full compliance moving forward."*

> *"Are hedging violations appealable? — No. Confirmed hedging violations are final and not eligible
> for appeal."*

> *"Accounts closed for hedging violations are not eligible for payouts, and any associated profits
> cannot be withdrawn."*

**T10 — tracked at trader level, not account level:**

> *"Hedging attempts are tracked at the **Trader level**. If a Trader received a first-time warning
> and acknowledged the hedging policy, subsequent hedging activity in **any newly purchased account**
> is treated as a post-acknowledgment violation."*

**T11 — the enforcement ladder** (which the July 2026 rewrite introduced, and which lane 02's record
does not carry): first detection → real-time modal with *"a brief window to un-hedge"*, positions
liquidated if not cured, **trading may continue**; same-day repeat → liquidation, no timer; next
trading day → forced acknowledgement (*type "I agree"*) before trading; thereafter → immediate
liquidation; excessive → **Temporary Hedging Violation**, barred for the remainder of the day. Then:

> *"after **numerous** warnings, your account may be permanently closed without further notice if you
> continue to hedge your positions. **This action is irreversible.**"*

**T12 — best practice, in Topstep's own words:**

> *"Trade a single account to completely avoid the possibility of hedging"* · *"Don't coordinate
> positions across accounts (or other traders) to offset risk"* · *"you're still fully responsible for
> all activity across your accounts, including anything created by automated systems or third-party
> tools"*

**T13 — Prohibited Conduct help article**
([10296582](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/10296582-prohibited-conduct), read 2026-09-08):

> *"**Coordinated trading** — performing trades in concert with others (including unconnected accounts
> or third parties) to pool risk, hedge aggregate positions, or trade the same or opposite strategy
> simultaneously"*

> *"**Cross-account hedging (single-user)** — holding opposite positions across multiple accounts
> simultaneously"*

> *"**Account stacking** — repeatedly hitting the Maximum Loss Limit in one account and switching to
> another to repeat high-risk attempts"*

**Note the drafting asymmetry.** The *coordinated trading* bullet — the one requiring a second
person — is the one that names *"third parties"*. The *single-user* bullet says only *"multiple
accounts"*, unqualified. **Read against T1 and T8, "multiple accounts" means multiple Topstep
accounts.**

**Arbitration (§22):** Delaware law; Court of Chancery / D. Del.; JAMS Comprehensive Rules,
Wilmington; class waiver. **Nothing about conduct at third parties.**

### 3.4 Take Profit Trader

**Method: the public Zendesk Help Center API returned all 87 articles in one call. Every article body
was extracted and searched.** This is the strongest negative in the lane because it is exhaustive
rather than sampled.

**P1 — Rule 6, opening sentence** ([Rule 6: No Counter
Positions](https://takeprofittraderhelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/30331694826909-Rule-6-No-Counter-Positions),
`edited_at` **2025-11-27**):

> *"Take Profit Trader strictly prohibits holding opposite positions in the same or closely related
> products **across any accounts under the same beneficial control**. This rule protects the integrity
> of our trading environment, ensures compliance with CME regulatory standards, and preserves our

### 3.5 FTMO — the only firm that engaged the question

**Primary source retained in-repo:
[`data/ftmo_challenge_terms_2026-08-04.txt`](../../../data/ftmo_challenge_terms_2026-08-04.txt)
(FTMO Challenge Terms and Conditions, 2026-08-04). Line numbers below are that file's.**

**F1 — Forbidden Trading Practice #2**
([ftmo.com/en/forbidden-trading-practices](https://ftmo.com/en/forbidden-trading-practices/), read
2026-09-08, **fetched raw and verified character-for-character, not via summariser**):

> *"perform, alone or in concert with other persons — including between connected accounts, **accounts
> held with various operators/providers**, or accounts held with other members of the Program Group —
> simulated trades or combinations of trades for manipulative purposes, for example by simultaneously
> entering into opposite positions (with the exception of entering into such positions on a single
> simulated account);"*

**This is the finding of the lane.** *"Accounts held with various operators/providers"* can only mean
accounts at other prop firms / other platform operators. **No other firm of the five uses comparable
words.** Note the carve-out at the end: opposite positions **on a single simulated account** are
exempt — so FTMO tolerates intra-account hedging while banning the cross-account and cross-provider
forms. That is the exact inverse of MFFU's rule (M6), which bans intra-account hedging outright.

**F2 — Forbidden Trading Practice #8, the day-distribution rule:**

> *"use trading strategies that artificially distribute profit across multiple days without
> proportionally distributing market risk, **such as hedging or holding opposing positions on the same
> or highly correlated instruments**, or partially closing and managing the same trade idea across
> multiple trading days, in order to circumvent the Best Day Rule"*

**F3 — the aggregate capital cap (Terms 5.8.5(c), line 481):**

> *"procure that the aggregate amount of the Initial Simulated Capital on all accounts held by you or
> parties affiliated with you, individually or in combination, **on all accounts with us and/or
> members of the FTMO Group** does not exceed USD 400,000."*

**F4 — the aggregation keys (Terms 6.3, line 538):**

> *"The total sum of the Initial Simulated Capital is calculated **in aggregate per client, per
> strategy and per ultimate beneficial owner or executive of another client or legal entity.**"*

**F5 — the definition that bounds F3 and F4 (line 1212):**

> *"**"FTMO Group"** means FTMO Evaluation, FTMO Trading and their respective affiliates."*

**Reading:** F4's *"per strategy"* is the most aggressive aggregation key in the sector — it caps a
**strategy**, not merely a person. **But F5 bounds it to FTMO's own entities.** The cap does not
reach a competitor's account. Corroborated in FTMO's own FAQ (*How many accounts can I have?*, read
2026-09-08):

> *"There is no limit to the number of accounts you may have. However, a maximum capital allocation
> limit applies. At any given time, the total capital allocation across all accounts is limited to
> **$400,000 per trader or strategy** (prior to any scaling)."* · *"Please note that holding multiple
> accounts through **different registrations** is not permitted. If **identically traded strategies**
> are detected across **multiple FTMO accounts** and the total fictitious capital exceeds the maximum
> capital allocation limit, FTMO reserves the right to suspend the affected accounts."*

**F6 — the Risk Management Rules, and the phrase that is NOT cross-firm (Terms 7.5, lines ~615):**

> *"7.5.1 opening substantially larger position sizes compared to your other simulated trades, **on
> any of your accounts**; 7.5.2 opening substantially smaller or larger number of positions compared
> to your other simulated trades, **regardless of which of your accounts are concerned**, and 7.5.3
> undertaking repeated simulated trading activity that results in higher Risk per Trade Idea…"*

**"Your accounts" here means your FTMO accounts** — 7.6 to 7.8 apply the consequences only to *"all
FTMO Challenge Accounts you have"* and *"all such involved accounts **with us**"*.

**F7 — intra-group information sharing (Terms 7.6.4):**

> *"inform FTMO Trading who may take applicable steps under the FTMO Account Terms and Conditions in
> respect of any FTMO Accounts you may have, including cancellation of all of your FTMO Accounts or
> rewards thereunder"*

**F8 — cross-account contagion (Terms 7.8):**

> *"If some or all Forbidden Trading Practices are executed on one or more of your FTMO Challenge
> Accounts, **or accounts of various related customers**, or by combining trading through FTMO
> Challenge Accounts and FTMO Accounts, then we are entitled to cancel all Services and terminate all
> Agreements relating to all FTMO Challenge Accounts you have, with immediate effect."*

**Note the asymmetry that matters most.** F1 makes conduct at *another provider's* account a
**violation**. F7 and F8 confine the **remedy** to accounts *"with us"*. **FTMO claims jurisdiction
over the act and no jurisdiction over the other firm's account — which is exactly what you would
expect, and which means the enforceable consequence of a cross-firm hedge is the loss of the FTMO
side.**

**F9 — the discretion, and the without-cause exit:**

> *"We reserve the right to determine, **at our own discretion**, whether certain simulated trades,
> practices, strategies, or situations qualify as or fall within a Forbidden Trading Practice. We may
> update the list of Forbidden Trading Practices from time to time."* (7.3)

> *"**Either party may terminate the Agreement without cause** by serving a written notice at least
> five (5) business days in advance on the other party."* (13.2.4)

**F10 — related-client aggregation on ORDER COUNT (Terms 3.14.1):**

> *"The total number of Orders may be limited in aggregate in case **other clients connected to you**
> are using the Services, specifically, if you are (i) the ultimate beneficial owner of such client,
> (ii) members of top management or another body with the highest executive authority of such client,
> or (iii) employee or officer of such client."*

**Also scoped to FTMO's own clients.**

**Personal-use rules (7.2.1–7.2.3)** prohibit third-party access to your FTMO accounts and trading in
coordination with third parties on FTMO accounts. **No disclosure obligation about other firms
anywhere in the document.** Clause 1.4 requires you to *"notify us immediately"* only if you cease to
meet FTMO's own eligibility criteria, which do not include holding accounts elsewhere.

**Dispute resolution:** Czech Trade Inspection Authority ADR (§17) and the EU ODR platform, plus §18
governing law. **Nothing about conduct at third parties.**

F7 both cross-reference — remains available only as a *"sample of the contract"* on request
(`SOURCES.md`, lane 05). **The cross-firm clause in the funded-phase document, if it differs, cannot

## 4. What is genuinely unaddressed

**These are conclusions, and each is a negative that was actively searched for.**

1. **No firm of the five prohibits, or requires disclosure of, simply holding a funded account at a
   competitor.** Not one. This was searched at the terms level, the help-centre level and, at TPT,
   exhaustively across all 87 articles. **The right answer to "is it allowed?" at four of five firms
   is "the question was never asked", not "yes".**

2. **No firm's aggregate-exposure language extends beyond its own perimeter.** FTMO's USD 400,000 cap
   is the only capital-level aggregate in the sector and it is bounded by *"FTMO Group"*, a defined
   term. Every other cap is per-account contracts or a count of accounts. **No firm caps, or claims
   to observe, a trader's total industry-wide exposure.**

3. **No arbitration or dispute clause among the five reaches conduct at a third party.** Five
   ordinary individual-arbitration-plus-class-waiver clauses (Collin County TX / Wilmington DE /
   Travis County TX / unspecified administrator / Czech ADR). **Nothing consolidates, nothing
   cross-references another firm's proceedings, nothing conditions eligibility on a competitor's
   determination.**

4. **Same-direction replication of one strategy across firms is prohibited by none of the five in
   writing.** TPT states affirmatively that syncing your own accounts is permitted (P6). FTMO's F1
   attaches to *manipulative purposes* and opposite positions, not to identical replication; F2
   attaches to artificial day-distribution. **The binding constraints on a replicated book are
   per-firm ones — contract caps, consistency rules, drawdown — not a cross-firm prohibition.**

5. **Two-sided books are the exposed case, and the exposure is asymmetric across the five.** FTMO
   prohibits it by name (F1). TPT's Rule 6 catches it textually (P1) while its own UTP scoping
   sentence contradicts that (P5) — **unreconciled**. Topstep's contract does not (T1/T2) but its
   help centre appears to (T7), **also unreconciled**, and Topstep's penalty is the harshest of the
   five (T9). Apex's wording is ambiguous (A1) and its governing agreement is unreadable. MFFU's is
   cleanly scoped and does not (M1–M3).

6. **Three of five governing contracts are not public.** MFFU's Simulated Trader Agreement (ranked
   *above* the Terms by MFFU's own §29), Apex's Evaluation and Performance Account User Agreement
   (*"shall prevail"* per Apex's own ToU), and FTMO's FTMO Account Terms and Conditions. **Any
   cross-firm clause could sit in any of the three and this lane would not see it.** The Apex case is
   the worst: 403 on the live host **and** zero Internet Archive snapshots.

7. **Nobody says they can see the other firm's book.** No firm of the five claims, in any document
   read, the ability to observe positions at a competitor. FTMO's remedy clauses (F7, F8) confine
   themselves to accounts *"with us"*. **Detection of a cross-firm hedge would require either
   self-report or inference from one side alone.** That is a statement about the documents, not an
   assurance — and it is the correct level of confidence to hold it at.

8. **The one thing worth carrying forward as a live risk.** Topstep's help centre and TPT's Rule 6
   both publish wording broader than their own governing scope, in the two documents a trader is most
   likely to rely on, at the two firms whose stated penalties are respectively **final and
   non-appealable** (T9) and **immediate liquidation plus profit forfeiture** (P3). **A two-sided
   multi-firm book is not defended by the contract text; it is defended only by the firm never
   forming a view about it.** That is not a constraint that can be modelled — it is an unpriced
   discretionary tail, and it belongs in the D379 valuation as one, not as zero.
