# 06 — Regulatory and litigation

**Lane 06 of the Prop-Firm-080926 research.** Accessed **2026-09-08** throughout; every source
carries its own access date and document date in [§Sources](#sources).

**Evidence discipline used here.** Four states are kept distinct and never merged:

| state | meaning |
|---|---|
| **ALLEGATION** | asserted in a complaint, notice of motion or warning. Proves nothing. |
| **FINDING** | a court or tribunal decided it, on a stated standard of proof |
| **SETTLEMENT** | resolved by consent; typically no admission |
| **DISMISSAL** | the claim ended. **Say on what ground** — merits, or something else entirely |

The single most important thing in this file is that the sector's one landmark case ended in a
**DISMISSAL on a ground that is not the merits.** Nearly every secondary write-up gets this wrong in
one direction or the other.

## VERDICT — what the regulatory record actually establishes

**1. No adjudicated finding anywhere says the funded-account business model is fraudulent. No
adjudicated finding anywhere says it is lawful either. The merits have never been reached.**

The CFTC sued the largest FX prop firm in the world (My Forex Funds / Traders Global) in August 2023.
That case was **dismissed with prejudice on 2025-05-13 as a sanction against the CFTC** for its own
litigation misconduct, and the agency was ordered to pay the defendants' costs. The dismissal
adjudicated the CFTC's conduct, **not** the defendants'. Anyone citing this case as vindication of
the prop model, or as proof of fraud, is misreading it. Both readings are wrong.

**2. But the record does establish, from regulators' own mouths and from a live judicial finding,
what the product actually IS. This is the part with real evidentiary value.**

Three independent regulators — on three continents, in three languages, none of them relying on the
others — describe the same mechanism:

- **The customer does not trade.** Belgium's FSMA (2024-03-07, primary): *"The consumer never makes
  any actual trades. He or she plays on a game of shadow investment."*
- **The firm chooses what, if anything, to mirror into the real market.** FSMA again: *"The prop
  trading firm alone decides under what conditions consumers may use their platform, and what
  simulated transactions it will copy."*
- **The firm's revenue comes from customers who fail.** FSMA: *"There is a good chance that some
  consumers never pass the courses. This is how prop trading firms earn money from them."*
- **The evaluation's difficulty is itself the complaint.** Italy's CONSOB (2024-07-08, primary)
  reports user complaints about *"il livello di difficoltà dei test, che sarebbero congegnati per
  spingere i «giocatori» a ritentare"* — the difficulty level of the tests, **allegedly engineered to
  push the "players" to retry** — and about *"la mancata condivisione dei presunti profitti"*, the
  non-payment of supposed profits.
- **The counterparty is the house.** Judge Quraishi, on a **prima facie** standard, enjoined Traders
  Global from *"acting as a counterparty to customers in retail forex or retail commodity
  transactions"* (2023-11-14). The CFTC's complaint had alleged Traders Global — not any third-party
  "liquidity provider" — was counterparty to substantially all customer trades.

For D379's purposes this is the load-bearing point, and it is stronger than any marketing page: the
prop account is **not** a claim on a real trading position. It is a **contractual payout obligation of
the firm, conditioned on the firm's own rule set, adjudicated by the firm.** The firm is short the
option it sold you and is also the exchange, the clearer and the umpire. D379's down-and-out call
framing is correct in form; the regulatory record adds that **the barrier is administered by the
counterparty**, which is a materially worse position than an exchange-traded barrier option.

**3. There is a genuine, unresolved legal split on what the product is — and it is split by
jurisdiction, not by fact pattern.** The same product is characterised four different ways:

| characterisation | who says it | status |
|---|---|---|
| **Not a regulated activity at all** — simulated trading on a demo account cannot be an investment service | **Czech National Bank (ČNB)** — FTMO's home regulator | stated position, 2024-10 |
| **May be a MiFID investment service**, depending on set-up | ČNB (2024-06), Simont Braun analysis | fact-dependent |
| **A security — an "investment contract"** under Ontario law | **Ontario Securities Commission** | ALLEGATION, 2024-07-05 |
| **Off-exchange retail forex/commodity transactions requiring registration** | **CFTC** | ALLEGATION, dismissed 2025 |

The ČNB position matters disproportionately because **FTMO is Czech-domiciled** (lane 05's control
firm). Its home regulator has effectively said the demo-account model needs no licence. That is not a
loophole the industry invented; it is the supervisor's stated reading.

**4. The industry is voluntarily walking INTO the US perimeter — which is itself evidence about
where the perimeter sits.** Topstep registered with the NFA as a Swap Firm and was approved as a
**Commodity Trading Advisor** (2026, NFA ID# 0567079 for Topstep Advisory LLC); Tradeify launched a
CFTC-regulated Introducing Broker ("Slay Markets"); MyFundedFutures has signalled the same. Firms do
not take on CTA obligations for fun. Read this as the operators' own assessment that the unregulated
posture is not durable in the US.

**5. Private litigation over payout denial is far thinner than the forums suggest — and the three
cases most often cited as "prop firm class actions" are not that.** I checked the dockets:

- *Footlick v. Topstep LLC* — an **employment and LLC-operating-agreement dispute** brought by former
  employees. Cause of action: **42:2000 Job Discrimination (Sex)**. Nothing to do with funded traders.
- *Riot v. Apex Trader Funding* — a **copyright and software-licence dispute** (17:501, NOS 820).
  Nothing to do with funded traders.
- *FPFX Technologies v. The Funded Trader* — a **vendor contract** dispute between a tech supplier
  and a prop firm.

The one real trader-versus-firm federal complaint I could confirm is *McCrudden v. Topstep*,
**3:26-cv-00816-WWB-LLL, filed 2026-04-09, pro se** — allegations only, untested, by a plaintiff with
his own regulatory history. **There is no certified class action against any prop firm over payout
denial that I could locate.** Given ~135,000 customers at one firm alone, the absence is a finding in
itself: these disputes are absorbed by arbitration clauses, ToS, and attrition, not litigated.

**6. Mass payout failure is real and documented, but the documentation is weak.** The 2024 cluster —
The Funded Trader (paused 2024-03-28), True Forex Funds (closed 2024-05-13), SurgeTrader (closed
2024-05-24) — is consistently reported across trade press, but the loss figures in circulation
($2M+ denied at TFT; ~$1.2M owed to ~300 traders at TFF; ~30% of SurgeTrader payouts processed) trace
to aggregator blogs, **not to any insolvency filing or court record I could reach.** Treat the
direction as established and the magnitudes as unverified. The proximate trigger was a **commercial**
event, not a regulatory one: MetaQuotes withdrawing MT4/MT5 licences from prop firms in early 2024.

**7. A warning about this sector's secondary literature — it fabricates specific regulatory facts.**
Three checkable claims from SEO-tier sites collapsed on inspection:

- *"ESMA's 2026-02-24 statement brings funded-trader models inside CFD intervention measures."*
  **False.** I read the statement (ESMA35-243228190-8024). It is about **perpetual futures** and does
  not mention prop firms, funded accounts, simulated trading or evaluations at all.
- *"SEC filed enforcement actions against two prop firms on 2026-08-15."* **Unverifiable.** Traces to
  a single marketing blog; nothing on sec.gov. The only SEC "proprietary trading firm" action I found
  is *Kershner Trading Americas* (2024-05-20), a Reg M short-selling violation by a genuine market-
  making firm — unrelated to funded accounts.
- *"NFA Notice I-26-12 sets affiliate marketing standards for prop firms."* **False.** I-26-12
  concerns **fingerprint application processing.**

Lane 07 should treat this tier as adversarial. Anything sourced only to `track360.io`,
`theindustryspread.com`, `fxtrustscore.com` or `copilink.com` should be discarded unless independently
confirmed.

## Substantive findings

### 1. The My Forex Funds case: what actually happened, in order

This is the sector's only landmark, and it is routinely mis-cited. The sequence:

1. **2023-06-09 to 06-16** — CFTC staff reviewing bank records flag CAD $31.55M leaving a Traders
   Global account. An investigator writes: *"In 3/23 he transfers 31.5M in and out again. I don't know
   where it goes."* On 06-16 OSC staff send bank pages showing the debits coded **TXINS** and
   **TXBAL** — standard Canadian bank codes for tax payments. The CFTC attorney later conceded at the
   evidentiary hearing that this email put him *"on notice of at least the possibility that these
   [transfers] were tax payments"* and that he *"took no steps on [his] own to verify"* it.
2. **2023-08-17** — an OSC email states the transfers appear to be corporate tax instalments. Both
   the CFTC investigator and attorney received it.
3. **2023-08-24/28** — the complaint and the supporting declaration are filed. **Paragraph 29**
   describes the CAD $31.5M as *"Transfer to unidentified Kazmi account."* The Special Master later
   found this line **comprised 97% of the debits in that table.**
4. **2023-08-29** — ex parte SRO: full asset freeze and a temporary receiver.
5. **2023-09-05/07** — the OSC files a sworn affidavit in Canada saying the transfers *"appear to be
   for corporation tax instalments."* The CFTC received it 09-07; the investigator did not download it
   until 09-20; the attorney never did.
6. **2023-09-22** — the CFTC's opposition brief **re-cites** the false paragraph as evidence of
   dissipation. The attorney testified he included it because he *"hoped it would influence the
   judge's decision."*
7. **2023-09-26** — defendants spell the error out: *"A simple Google search would have revealed
   this."* The CFTC conceded it knew by this date at the latest. **It still did not correct the
   record before the PI hearing.**
8. **2023-10-31** — the CFTC files a corrective disclosure for a *different* declaration, to fix a
   typo and attach a missing exhibit. The Special Master drew the obvious inference: the agency *"knew
   how to make a corrective disclosure and chose not to"* for the material one.
9. **2023-11-06** — PI hearing. The investigator testifies he learned the truth *after* filing.
   The Special Master found this **false**.
10. **2023-12-01** — the CFTC finally discloses the August email — **in a footnote on page 5 of a
    reply brief on the receiver's fee motion.** *"[T]he CFTC chose obfuscation over clarity and
    transparency."*
11. **2025-04-30 / 05-13** — sanctions recommended and adopted; dismissal with prejudice; fees.

**What this does and does not establish.** It establishes serious misconduct by a US financial
regulator, judicially found, at a cost of >$3.1M. It establishes **nothing whatever** about whether
Traders Global defrauded anyone. The Special Master reached no merits question; the pending motion to
dismiss was denied **as moot**. Note also the OSC's pointed line in its own filing: the US court's
rollback was influenced by *"an error in the CFTC's evidence … **an error that does not exist in the
Commission's evidence.**"* The Canadian allegations were never contaminated by the CFTC's mistake and
have never been withdrawn — though they have also never been tested, and **no Statement of
Allegations was ever issued.**

### 2. The regulatory characterisation question — genuinely open, and split by domicile

Firms characterise themselves as providers of **evaluation / education / simulated-trading services**.
Finance Magnates records that after the MFF action and the MetaQuotes crackdown, firms *"overnight
changed the language on their websites to clarify that activities are 'simulated'."* That is a
litigation-driven relabelling, and it should be read as such — but it also happens to match what the
FSMA independently found the mechanism to be.

The regulators do not agree with each other:

- **ČNB (Czech)** — the most permissive, and the one that governs FTMO. Simulated trading on a demo
  account with virtual funds *"cannot meet the characteristics of the provision of investment
  services"* and is *"not an activity subject to a CNB permit."* Real-account models may fall under
  MiFID.
- **FSMA (Belgium)** — does not assert illegality; states prop firms *"do not hold any authorisation
  and therefore are not allowed to provide investment services"*, and warns. The distinction is
  perimeter-descriptive, not an enforcement position.
- **CONSOB (Italy)** — warning only. Frames the product as a **game**, which implicitly places it
  outside the investment-services perimeter while still treating it as a consumer-harm problem.
- **OSC (Ontario)** — the most aggressive: alleges each account **was itself a security** — an
  "investment contract" under s.1(1)(n) — which if sustained would make the whole industry an
  unregistered securities distribution. **Never adjudicated.**
- **CFTC** — alleged off-exchange leveraged retail forex/commodity transactions requiring
  registration. Enforcement Director McGinley in 2023: *"Anyone offering or entering into leveraged
  retail forex contracts without registration … is acting in clear violation of the law."* That
  theory died with the case, on a procedural ground, and has not been re-tested.

**The legal reasoning that actually matters** (Simont Braun, 2024-05-21): dealing on own account is
not a regulated investment service *"in so far no actual investment services to clients are offered"*
— **but** regulation attaches *"as soon as the dealing on own account is in reality part of a
back-to-back trading set-up for the benefit of clients."* Whether a given prop firm is inside or
outside therefore turns on **whether customer orders are mirrored to the real market**, which is
precisely the fact the FSMA says the firm alone controls and does not disclose. The perimeter test
depends on a fact only the firm knows.

### 3. Business-model detail that only the filings contain

Facts of this specificity appear in no marketing material. **All are allegations unless marked.**

| fact | source | state |
|---|---|---|
| ≥135,000 customers signed up since Nov 2021 | CFTC complaint | ALLEGATION |
| ≥$310M paid in fees (CFTC) / ≥USD 294M raised (OSC) | CFTC PR 8771-23; OSC NoM ¶5(e) | ALLEGATION (two regulators, ~5% apart) |

| The firm **"actively minimizes the likelihood that customers trade profitably"** via pretextual terminations, misleading commissions, software causing worse fills than displayed, and **"handicapping the extremely small number of successful customers"** | CFTC complaint | ALLEGATION |
| Firm may have paid simulated "profits" to investors **out of other investors' money** | OSC NoM ¶5(f) | ALLEGATION |
| Each funded account **was a security / investment contract** | OSC NoM ¶5(g) | ALLEGATION |
| The consumer **never makes any actual trades**; the firm **alone decides which simulated transactions it will copy**; firms **earn money from consumers who never pass** | FSMA warning | REGULATOR'S DESCRIPTION of the sector (not case-specific) |
| Evaluations allegedly **engineered to force retries**; profits allegedly not shared | CONSOB, from user reports | REGULATOR'S REPORT of complaints |

**The phrase to carry into D379 is the OSC's:** *rules "designed to benefit TGG to the detriment of
investors."* That is a regulator asserting the **barrier geometry itself is the revenue mechanism** —
which is exactly the object D379 is trying to price. It remains an allegation. But it is the correct
prior for reading any firm's published rule set: the drawdown convention, the consistency rule and the
payout ladder are **not** risk controls incidentally borne by the customer; on this theory they are the
product.

### 4. Private litigation — thinner than reputation, and mostly mislabelled

Docket-verified (I read the dockets, not the write-ups):

- ***Footlick v. Topstep LLC*, 1:22-cv-06152 (N.D. Ill.), filed 2022-11-07.** Cause: **42:2000 Job
  Discrimination (Sex)**; Nature of Suit: Contract: Other. Six plaintiffs, **former
  employees/members**, disputing the 2016 and 2020 Operating Agreements. Partial dismissal 2024-03-26
  (Counts VIII–XI dismissed entirely). Topstep counterclaimed against four plaintiffs. One plaintiff
  (Simkins) dismissed with prejudice 2024-05-28. **Still live**: summary-judgment conference set
  **2026-10-08**. Spawned two related state actions — *Topstep LLC v. Patel* (Cook County
  2026L003425) and *Topstep LLC v. Footlick* (Del. Ch. 2026-0387-KSJM). **Not a trader case.**
- ***Riot v. Apex Trader Funding Inc.*, 1:24-cv-01557 (W.D. Tex.), filed 2024-12-18.** Cause:
  **17:501 Copyright Infringement**, NOS 820. Software Licence Agreement, termination letter, TRO,
  counterclaims and third-party claims against Daytraders.com and MKNET LLC. Active as of 2026-08-20.
  **Not a trader case.**
- ***FPFX Technologies v. The Funded Trader / Easton Consulting*** — tech vendor suing over unpaid
  dues and a $500,000 exclusivity-breach clause. **Not a trader case.**
- ***McCrudden v. Topstep*, 3:26-cv-00816-WWB-LLL (M.D. Fla.), filed 2026-04-09, pro se.** The only
  confirmed trader-versus-prop-firm federal complaint. **Allegations only.** I was unable to read the
  docket (CourtListener rate-limited); the quoted language comes from secondary reporting of the
  filing.

**No certified class action over payout denial was located in any jurisdiction.** Combined with
~135,000 customers at a single firm, this says the disputes are being resolved outside court —
arbitration clauses, terms-of-service, and abandonment. For D379 that is a **cost of enforcement**
point: the payout obligation is, in practice, weakly enforceable by the individual customer.

### 5. Collapses and payout failures — direction solid, magnitudes unverified

The 2024 cluster is consistently reported but **documentarily thin**. I found no insolvency filing,
receivership order or court record for any of them.

| firm | event | date | claimed shortfall | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Funded Trader | paused all operations; later relocated to Cayman Islands | 2024-03-28 | >$2M denied payouts; 1,272 people awaiting funds | aggregator/trade press only |
| True Forex Funds | permanent closure, cited insolvency | 2024-05-13 | ~$1.2M owed to ~300 traders | aggregator/trade press only |
| SurgeTrader | ceased operations | 2024-05-24 | ~30% of owed payouts processed by 2024-08 | aggregator/trade press only |
| sector-wide | ~80–100 firms ceased operating, Feb 2024 – late 2025 | 2024–25 | — | aggregator estimate, **uncorroborated** |

**The trigger was commercial, not regulatory:** MetaQuotes withdrawing MT4/MT5 licences from prop
firms (early 2024) — the same event that caused FTMO to stop onboarding US clients. **No CFTC order
compelled FTMO to exit the US**; that is a common misreading.

This still matters for D379 **§4**: it is direct evidence that the payout obligation carries
**counterparty credit risk**, and that this risk was realised at scale within a two-year window. The
account is not merely a down-and-out call — it is a down-and-out call **written by an unregulated,
unbonded, thinly-capitalised counterparty with no client-money segregation.** Any valuation that
ignores the issuer's default probability is overstating the instrument.
