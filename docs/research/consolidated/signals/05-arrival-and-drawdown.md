## Concentration in time — the premise was wrong and the finding is real `[EXT]` `D4`

**The commissioner built a lane on `C4`'s remark that three of 132 months carry half the total.**

> **It is not a measurement of arrival.** An iid Gaussian null at the same `T` and `t` gives a median
> of **4**. **The market's own equity premium is at 2.31% against `C4`'s 2.27%.** All thirteen series
> measured fall in a band. *"I over-read a passing remark."*

**But arrival IS clustered, and the statistic should have been looking at clustering rather than
concentration:**

| | |
|---|---|
| share of the long leg's total in **twelve months** | **73.7%**, `p` = 0.003 |
| what the market did in that window | **lost money** |
| the short leg's premium in the **same 36 months** | **108.7% of its own total**, `p` = 0.004 |

> **The commissioner's second wrong premise: he conjectured the two legs' bad months would offset.
> They do not — they COINCIDE. The spread COMPOUNDS the long leg's clustering rather than smoothing
> it.**

**And a benchmark catch `D4` was not sent to find flips a sign** (§8). Read it there.

## The drawdown of a long-only tilt — and nobody publishes it `[EXT]` `C3`

**`C3`'s reframing finding, taken first because it reframes everything else:** the two sources that
*appear* to report a long-only tilt's drawdown are reporting **a market-neutralised leg** (2.2% vol,
−0.02 beta) and **a column headed *"% cumulative underperformance"***. **Neither is the object.**

**What `C3` then measured itself**, with every control stated, and a **block-bootstrap null** nobody
commissioned:

| | |
|---|---|
| the EW tilt in this programme's era | **10.62% against the universe's 10.66%** |
| its drawdown against the universe's | **−38.60% vs −36.42% — DEEPER** |
| time to `t` = 2 at +0.026 %/mo | **1,235 years** |

**Sub-questions `C3` answers and this page does not restate:** are profitability's worst episodes
value's? does the diversification survive the moment it is needed? is the tilt's drawdown
distinguishable from the market's?

**And the conflict `C3` dissolves without discarding either side `[inside-brief]`:** two sources give
**opposite orderings** for whether the long leg or the spread drawdowns worse (−8.2% vs −3.9% one way,
−10.5% vs −24.2% the other). **`C3` shows mean drawdown is a Sharpe statistic and neither paper says
so** — then leaves both readings on the record.

## What the repo already knows about holding `[REPO]`

- **[FINDINGS §25](../../../FINDINGS.md):** the hold is a **cost lever**, and under a target exit `k`
  is a **cap, not a hold**.
- **`CLAUDE.md`:** *cost-cutting ≠ edge-sharpening* — a longer hold lifts breakeven by amortising one
  round trip while per-bar edge usually **falls**, so Sharpe can drop as cost coverage rises.
- **[FINDINGS §36](../../../FINDINGS.md):** on a refilled slot book the target fires first.

**None of this contradicts the external material — it is the same trade-off measured on a horizon two
orders of magnitude shorter.** A tilt held in **months** and a book held in **bars** do not share a
cost curve.
