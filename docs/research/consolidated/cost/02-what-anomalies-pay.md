# What published anomalies actually pay — and why cost stopped being the answer

**The single most consequential reversal in the research record, and it corrects a premise the
commissioner wrote into the prompt himself.**

> `[EXT]` `K6` (R6): **for the anomalies that actually survive post-2005, COST IS NOT THE BINDING
> CONSTRAINT. And this programme's 33.8 bp/side is NOT above the cost-honest literature — it IS that
> literature's own number.**

## The measured cost level `[EXT]`

| | |
|---|---|
| average effective spread paid by **204 academic anomaly implementations**, post-2005 | **68 bp**, charged half per trade = **34 bp/side** |
| the same measure for the **average NYSE stock** | **16 bp** — against **67 bp** actually paid |
| survivors' one-sided turnover | **1.2–7.2%/month**, annually rebalanced |
| **what that costs at 33.8 bp/side** | **~1.4 bp/month** |

**The factor of four between 16 and 67 is not a defect — it is what an anomaly portfolio trades.**
And at 1.4 bp/month, **cost cannot be what kills an annually rebalanced signal.**

**`[REPO]` cross-check:** 33.8 vs 34 is [agreement A1](../conflicts/03-agreements.md); a second,
independent route via the equal-weight bias gives **34.5** ([A2](../conflicts/03-agreements.md)).
**But read [vs repo R1](../conflicts/02-versus-repo-measurements.md) first — 33.8 is a superseded
statistic here.**

## The sentence that reframes the whole programme `[EXT]`

> **Of the ten strongest post-2005 survivors: seven need Compustat fundamentals, two of the three
> price-only ones are on this programme's own exclusion list, and one needs options.
> ZERO are computable from permitted free daily price and volume data.**

**This is not a claim that nothing is tradeable** — `K6` says so itself. It is a claim about *the
published survivors*. What `K6` could extract is a **shape**, and the shape is the deliverable:

- low turnover, holds in **months**
- a **persistent characteristic**, not a dated event
- a short leg
- **≥ 20 names per leg**
- a turnover × illiquidity interaction under which **costs are immaterial even in microcaps for
  annually rebalanced signals**

**That describes a different kind of strategy from anything this programme has built** — and it is
why `Scan-100926` attacks the *constraints* rather than hunting a twelfth territory.

## The cost-level conflict, unadjudicated `[CONFLICT C13]`

Frazzini–Israel–Moskowitz report far lower costs than Novy-Marx–Velikov. **`K6` resolves it by
quoting one side's own text**: their number describes a **patient** trader in a microcap-free
universe, while TAQ-based measures capture *"retail traders, liquidity demanders, and impatient
traders."* **Both readings stand.**

## Survival is close to random, and the free draft says the opposite `[EXT]` `K6`

**No observable feature reliably predicts which anomalies survive.** And inside that finding, a trap:

> **A working-paper draft reports that turnover reliably predicts post-2005 net returns. The
> PUBLISHED version says no predictor is reliable and that turnover enters WITH THE WRONG SIGN. THE
> DRAFT IS THE FREELY-INDEXED ONE.**

**The only feature that does predict persistence — being expensive and hard to arbitrage — is the
same feature that makes an effect untradeable here.**

**`K6`'s own largest fragility, self-flagged:** one author writes four of its load-bearing sources and
they share one dataset. **The post-2005 collapse is corroborated across cuts but not by an
independent group with independent portfolio construction.**
