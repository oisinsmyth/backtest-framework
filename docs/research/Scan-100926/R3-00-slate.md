## Why these four

**Round 2 ended with one computable candidate and four unanswered questions about it.** Gross
profitability is the family that survives both the evidence and the tag collapse. Round 3 asks the
four things that stand between *"computable"* and *"worth measuring"* — **and not one of them is a
new signal.**

**`C1` exists because `B2` flagged its largest fact and did not resolve it.** *"The quarterly variant
is 2–4× stronger in three measurements and natively point-in-time from XBRL — the lane's largest
unexploited fact, flagged not resolved."* And it collides with round 1's residual: `A1` measured that
**57.5% of recent 10-K and 10-Q submissions are accepted at or after 16:00 ET carrying that same
day's `filed` date**, rising from 39.3% in 2012q2 — **a same-session look-ahead in the majority of
cases for a book that trades the close.** `B2` documented the **annual** convention verbatim (a
rolling six-month lag). **Nobody has asked what lag a QUARTERLY panel needs**, and the two facts have
never been put in the same room.

**`C2` exists because round 2 discovered the same thing three times, independently, in three
different subject areas** — the **benchmark** moved `CbOP`'s long-leg *t* from 1.95 to 5.98; the
**deflator** moved gross profitability's *t* from 3+ to 1.04–1.85; the **session boundary** moves a
decomposition's sign. **Three lanes that were not talking to each other each found that the
construction detail nobody's name carries decides the answer.** The obvious next question is the one
none of them asked: **how big is that dispersion in general, and has anyone measured it?**

**`C3` exists because it is the statistic the books are actually judged on and no lane has ever asked
for it.** `drawdown` appears 33 times in `docs/` and **in this campaign it appears only as a number
quoted inside `A3`'s summary of one source** — never as a question. `CLAUDE.md` requires maxDD in
every reported result and the prop book's hurdle P turns on it. **A long-only characteristic tilt's
drawdown profile is unresearched here.**

**`C4` exists because the one computable family was published in 2013 and every headline number in
`B2`'s brief comes from a sample ending 2012–2015.** `A4` already quotes the field's own summary
figure — after spreads and post-publication decay, **the average anomaly's expected return is ~4
bp/month.** **If gross profitability has decayed to that, the whole round-2 result is a museum
piece**, and this is the question that precedes any build.

## `C1` — the quarterly variant, and the lag a filing-driven book must carry

**Sub-questions.** Which papers measure a **quarterly** profitability sort rather than an annual one,
what they report, and **on what samples** — reported separately, never pooled. The exact
construction: is the quarterly numerator a **single quarter, a trailing four-quarter sum, or a
year-over-year change**, and what is the denominator's timing? **Whether the 2–4× advantage `B2`
found survives the deflator convention `B2` also found** — because if the quarterly result is
measured with a current-asset deflator, it inherits the investment-signal contamination. What the
literature's **lag convention for quarterly accounting data** actually is, quoted verbatim, and
whether anyone justifies it rather than inheriting it. And the residual nobody has joined: given
`A1`'s **57.5%** same-day-`filed` measurement, **what is the smallest defensible lag for a book that
must decide before a close it may already be too late for** — and does any source measure the
premium's sensitivity to that lag?

**The bar.** Either a named quarterly construction with its lag, its evidence and its tag list — or
a finding that the quarterly advantage does not survive the constructions round 2 showed matter,
which would settle `B2`'s open flag in the other direction. **Both outcomes are the lane succeeding.**

**HARD SCOPE.** This is about **the accounting variable's frequency and its timing**. It is **NOT**
about earnings announcements, PEAD, earnings dates, or the earnings-release absorption census — all
permanently spent ground from the first campaign. If the research drifts to *"what happens around
the announcement"*, it is in the wrong lane.

## `C2` — how much does the answer move?

**Sub-questions.** Is there a literature that **measures the dispersion of results across reasonable
construction choices** — multiple teams given one dataset and one question, or one team enumerating a
construction grid? What dispersion do they report, **in the units a reader can use** (a range of
*t*-statistics, a range of alphas, a fraction of sign flips)? Which specific choices are documented
as **most** load-bearing, and do the three round 2 found independently — benchmark, deflator,
weighting — appear on that list? Whether anyone distinguishes **choices that are arbitrary** from
**choices that encode a different economic question**, because round 2 found at least one of each.
And the honest inversion: **if the dispersion across defensible constructions is wider than the
premium itself, what follows for a programme that will make every one of those choices once?**

**The bar.** A named, quantified dispersion with its source and method — **or an explicit finding
that the field does not measure this**, which would make round 2's three independent discoveries a
finding about the field rather than about three papers.

## `C3` — the shape of the drawdown

**Sub-questions.** What is documented about the **drawdown profile of a long-only characteristic
tilt** — depth, duration, and **time to recovery** — as distinct from a long–short factor's? Which
episodes are documented as the worst for **profitability specifically**, and are they the same
episodes as value's? What happens to the tilt's **correlation with the market during stress** — does
the diversification the tilt is supposed to supply survive the moment it is needed? Whether any
source reports drawdown for the **long leg alone** rather than the spread, since round 2 established
that the two are different objects. And the programme-facing question: **for a book whose benchmark
is cash, is the drawdown of a tilted long-only book distinguishable from the market's own, or is it
the market's drawdown with a small alpha attached?**

**The bar.** Named drawdown figures for a long-only characteristic tilt with their samples and
constructions — or a clean statement that the literature reports drawdowns **only for long–short
spreads**, which would itself be a finding, because it is the spread's drawdown that the programme
would otherwise have been reasoning from.

## `C4` — is it already dead?

**Sub-questions.** What is measured about **post-publication decay** — the headline estimates, their
samples, and **the counter-argument**, because there is a live dispute about whether decay is a real
economic effect or a statistical artefact of the original in-sample estimate. **For gross
profitability specifically:** what has it done since 2013, and on what samples — reported separately.
Whether any source distinguishes **decay from era-dependence**, since this programme's own records
show numbers inverting by era with no publication event to explain them. And the out-of-sample
evidence that is neither a backtest nor a paper: **have long-only, profitability-tilted funds
existed long enough to have a live track record**, and what does it show?

**The bar.** A dated, sourced answer on whether this specific family still pays post-publication —
or a finding that the evidence cannot separate decay from era-dependence, which would be the honest
answer and is worth as much.

**THE SALES-INSTRUMENT WARNING APPLIES HARDEST IN THIS LANE.** A fund's own marketing is **never
evidence for a return**. A fund's **filed NAV history** is primary data; a fund's **factsheet** is a
sales instrument. **Tag them differently, and say which you used for every number.** And name every
fund's **survivorship problem** — closed funds do not publish track records, and round 2 already
recorded two overnight ETFs that closed fourteen months after launch.
