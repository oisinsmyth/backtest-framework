Four basis points from the stop

Seven worked examples from the strategy exactly as taught — change of character, flipped level, 61.8% retracement, fair value gap, all four agreeing on one bar. Chosen by a rule fixed before any outcome was looked at. The worst trade in the book is not a bad prediction; it is a fee bill.

222 stacked trades D204 – D212 24 Aug 2026

What you are looking at

The full confluence arm, 15-minute bars, eight years

Every trade below comes from the arm the course actually teaches: a change of character, then a pullback where the flipped level, the 61.8% retracement and a fair value gap all hold at the same bar. 118 such trades on BTCUSDT and 104 on ETHUSDT over eight years of 15-minute bars.

The wrapper is identical in every one and never varies: stop at the origin of the impulse leg, target at 5R, a channel trail once 1R in profit, a 60-bar cap. Entry is a market order at the close of the signalling bar. Costs are 40 bps per side, so a round trip pays 80.

Two numbers set the frame. At the measured costs this arm needs a 40.2% hit rate on BTC and 33.5% on ETH just to break even at a 5R target. It achieves 11.0% and 15.4%.

How these seven were chosen

The rule, written down before the outcomes were read

Examples are the easiest thing in a study to cherry-pick, so the selection is mechanical and was fixed in the extraction script before anything was inspected: per symbol, the best, median and worst trade by net R; then, pooled across both symbols, the trades with the highest and lowest cost in R.

That names eight slots and yields seven trades, because the highest-cost trade in either book is BTC's worst. Which is itself the finding: on this rule the worst outcome and the most expensive one are the same event.

BTCUSDT

118 trades — best, median, worst

  • BEST The strategy working exactly as advertised — and the golden ratio is not really in it. A change of character, a 12-bar impulse down 2.06%, a pullback into a fair value gap 0.52 ATR wide, a stop 1.19 ATR away at the leg’s origin, and a clean 5R banked 38 bars later for +4.395R net. It is a good trade. But it filled at a 37.8% retracement, not 61.8%: the 0.618 level sat at 6,984.66 and price closed 33.70 below it. The touch band is half an ATR — 37.41 on a leg of 144.88, or 25.8% of the entire move. “Price touched the golden ratio” was true anywhere between the 36% and 88% retracement.
  • MEDIAN A textbook loss, correctly handled, and still half fee. Short at 54.2% into the leg, stop 2.10 ATR away, 5R target below. Price went +0.67R in favour, turned, and paid the stop 18 bars later. Nothing went wrong: gross -1.00R is exactly one unit of planned risk. What makes it a losing business is the line beneath — 0.802R of fees on that 1R, so the median trade hands back 80% of its risk before the market is consulted.
  • WORST The worst trade in the book is not a bad prediction. It is a fee bill. Gross it lost -1.00R — an ordinary stop-out, identical to the median. The difference is that the entry filled 6.22 from its stop on a 16,239.70 bitcoin: 0.038% of price, 0.08 ATR. An 80 bp round trip against a 3.8 bp stop is 20.891R, and no position size makes that tradeable. The setup was textbook: change of character, 57.8% retracement, inside a fair value gap 0.37 ATR wide, all three filters agreeing. They agreed on a trade whose entire risk was smaller than the spread. Its recorded +8.99R best excursion is the same tininess from the other side — the excursion window includes the entry bar, whose own fifteen minutes are nine times a six-dollar stop. At this scale every number in R is noise.

ETHUSDT

104 trades — best, median, worst

  • BEST The mirror of BTC’s best, five years later and the other way up. A long at 56.3% into a 6-bar impulse, a fair value gap 1.81 ATR wide, stop 2.54 ATR below at the leg’s origin, target reached in 20 bars for +4.154R. A real 5R, and the tightest the 61.8% band gets in this set — half an ATR here is only 9.6% of the leg, so the level meant something closer to what the course claims. The fee still took 0.846R. Fees are not why this trade fails, because it does not fail. They are why the 103 around it do.
  • MEDIAN Deeper than the golden ratio, and it made no difference. Entered at a 73.0% retracement — well past 61.8%, and still inside the band, which here reaches to 79%. Price never moved more than +0.18R in favour and stopped out in 3 bars. The wrapper did nothing wrong and the entry was simply not predictive. This is what the middle of 104 perfect-confluence setups looks like.
  • WORST The same defect as BTC’s worst, on a different asset in a different year. A 0.37% impulse leg — six dollars on a 1,635.15 ether — with the entry 0.64 from its stop, 0.039% of price. Cost 20.443R. That the two worst trades in two independent books are the same shape is the point: the leg’s origin can sit arbitrarily close to the pullback entry, and nothing anywhere in the rule prevents it. There is no minimum stop, because the course never mentions one.

The cheapest trade the rule ever produced

Everything about the cost structure going right

  • LOW When the fees are as small as they ever get, the trade makes a sixth of one unit of risk. A 4.74% impulse leg puts the stop 2.99 ATR away, so the round trip costs only 0.204R — the lowest of all 222 stacked trades in both books. Price drifted the right way, never came near the 5R target 19.7% above, and closed at the 60-bar cap for +0.367R gross. Net +0.163R. This is the ceiling of the friendly case.

Three things the examples show

Each visible in the numbers above, not inferred from them

  • 1 The stop can be arbitrarily tight and the rule does not care. Stop distance is the unretraced part of the leg, so a deep entry on a small leg leaves almost nothing between fill and stop. Across the two books that produces trades risking 0.038% and 0.039% of price, on which an 80 bp round trip is twenty times the risk. 67% of BTC’s stacked trades and 50% of ETH’s cost at least their entire risk to put on. A minimum-stop filter would fix it and would also be a new parameter the course never states.
  • 2 The 61.8% level is a band, and the band is up to a quarter of the leg on each side. The touch tolerance is half an ATR in price. On a 15-minute impulse leg that is 9.6% of the move at its tightest here and 28.1% at its loosest — so “price reached the golden ratio” can be satisfied anywhere from the 34% to the 90% retracement. It is why this filter admits roughly seven setups in ten, and why the best trade in the BTC book qualifies at a 37.8% retracement.
  • 3 The winners are real. There are not enough of them. Both best trades reached a clean, full 5R exactly as designed — this is not a strategy that never works. At the measured costs it needs 40.2% of trades to do that on BTC and 33.5% on ETH. It gets 11.0% and 15.4%. Over eight years the full confluence arm turns 10,000 into 3,352 on BTC and 4,321 on ETH, against buy-and-hold’s 56,000 and 21,521.

What these examples do not show

Seven trades cannot carry a verdict and are not being asked to. The verdict came from 222 stacked trades, 3,875 setups, eight parameter cells and a set of matched-placebo controls, and it is that no component of this strategy predicts anything once leg size relative to ATR is held constant. These are illustrations of a conclusion reached elsewhere.

Two of them flatter the strategy on purpose. The selection rule includes both best trades because a page that showed only losers would be answering a question nobody asked — and both of those are genuine 5R winners, correctly identified, correctly held to target.

And none of this refutes the course as a human practises it. What was tested is the mechanised version, which is not the thing being taught. A discretionary trader would have skipped the six-dollar stop. That instinct is exactly what mechanising the rules removed, and its absence from the written rules is the finding.
